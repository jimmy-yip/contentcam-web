ContentCam vs DualTake (2026)

Checked 11 November 2026 · Disclosure: I'm Jimmy and I build ContentCam

ContentCam and DualTake both save a 9:16 and a 16:9 file from one take, but only ContentCam records the front and back cameras at the same time. ContentCam runs on iPhone and Android, saves the front and back cameras as two separate files you can edit, adds manual exposure, ISO, shutter and white balance plus a built-in teleprompter, and never puts a watermark on your video on any plan. DualTake is iPhone only, records with one camera at a time, and watermarks free exports.

This page is about DualTake: Dual Camera Recorder from Block Browser Inc (App Store id6761738035, dualtakecam.com). Other apps use the DualTake name, including a front and back recorder at getdualtake.app from a different developer; none of them is ContentCam. Every DualTake claim below comes from its App Store listing and dualtakecam.com.

What are the main differences?

  • Front and back at once. ContentCam's Front & Back mode records the selfie camera and the rear camera at the same time and saves them as two separate files. DualTake's listing says "DualTake records with one camera"; you can flip between front and back during a take, but not record both.
  • Platforms. ContentCam is on iPhone and Android. DualTake is on the App Store and requires iOS 18; its own comparison page says it does not work on Android.
  • Watermark. ContentCam has none, free or Premium. DualTake's listing says Premium removes the watermark, so free exports carry one.
  • Free tier. ContentCam's free tier gives unlimited Single Lens recordings in portrait and landscape at once, in 1080p, plus 5 Dual Lens and 5 Front & Back recordings, with no ads and no watermark. DualTake's free takes are up to 1 minute at 1080p, with a watermark.
  • Vertical and horizontal at once. Both. DualTake's listing says one take gives a vertical 9:16 video and a horizontal 16:9 video, both landing in Photos.

Where does ContentCam pull ahead?

Two cameras, two files. For a reaction, a walk-and-talk or a cooking video, ContentCam records your face and the scene in one take, as two separate files. You can cut between full-screen face and full-screen scene in CapCut or DaVinci Resolve, grade each one separately, or run ContentCam's Combine editor for a picture-in-picture or split-screen version and keep the originals. DualTake records one camera at a time, so your face and the scene can't be filmed at the same moment.

Android. If your phone is an Android, or you work with someone who shoots on one, ContentCam runs on both. DualTake is iPhone only.

No watermark, free included. ContentCam adds no watermark and no ads on any plan, and gives 5 free Front & Back recordings before Premium. DualTake's free exports carry a watermark until you pay.

The full sensor frame. In Single Lens mode ContentCam records the camera's full 4:3 frame and cuts both formats from it, so the horizontal keeps the full width and the vertical keeps the full height. On iPhone 11 and later, Dual Lens records the wide and ultra-wide cameras at the same time, for two different framings of one scene.

Camera controls. ContentCam gives you manual exposure, white balance, ISO and shutter, plus a built-in teleprompter that scrolls over the camera view close to the lens and never appears in the recorded files.

What is DualTake built for?

DualTake is an iPhone app for talking-to-camera clips in two formats. Its listing says it records with one camera and cuts the horizontal file from the same frame as the vertical one, following your face, with a teleprompter that scrolls next to the lens. It lists up to 4K at 24, 30 or 60 fps, HDR, MOV or MP4, exposure control, zoom, a countdown timer, pause and resume, and photo mode, and says it has no account and no ads.

On price, the US App Store sells DualTake Premium as a weekly, monthly or yearly subscription, or as a lifetime purchase. Its listing says free takes are up to 1 minute at 1080p, and that Premium removes the time limit and the watermark and adds 4K, pro codecs, HDR, full zoom and exposure controls. DualTake has 4.4 stars from 18 US ratings. It was released on 10 April 2026 and version 3.9 was updated on 26 October 2026. The listing requires iOS 18.0 or later.

Does DualTake record 4K with both cameras?

No, because DualTake doesn't record two cameras at once. Its listing says "Up to 4K, at 24, 30 or 60 fps" and "DualTake records with one camera" (App Store listing, checked 11 November 2026), so its 4K is a single-camera figure, and both of its files are cut from that one frame. Apple only lets third-party apps record two cameras in formats it marks as multi-camera capable. On an iPhone 18 Pro that list includes 3840 x 2160 at 30 fps but no 4K format at 60 fps, so 4K at 60 fps can't come from two cameras recording at once. ContentCam's Single Lens records up to 4K from one camera, and its Front & Back records HD, as two separate files.
