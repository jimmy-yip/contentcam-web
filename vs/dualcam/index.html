ContentCam vs DualCam (2026)

Tested 12 November 2026 · Listing checked 12 November 2026 · Disclosure: I'm Jimmy and I build ContentCam

Tested: the biggest difference is how each app makes the horizontal video. DualCam's 16:9 file is a slice cut from the middle of the vertical picture and enlarged to 1920 x 1080, so it's no wider than the vertical video and has less real detail. ContentCam's Single Lens records the camera's full 4:3 frame and cuts each format from it, so the horizontal keeps the full width, the vertical keeps the full height, both keep their resolution, and it works in 4K at up to 60 fps. ContentCam also records 4K in Dual Lens (two rear lenses at once, each with its own framing) and with both cameras in Front & Back. DualCam has no Dual Lens mode, no 4K with both cameras (its own settings say 4K is single-camera only), no 60 fps in any mode I tried, and stops recording after 60 seconds until you pay. Full results and screenshots are below.

I'm Jimmy, I build ContentCam, and I tested DualCam myself on 12 November 2026. Everything marked as tested comes from that session; the rest comes from DualCam's App Store listing, its website and its privacy policy.

What are the main differences?

  • How the horizontal video is made. DualCam cuts the 16:9 file out of the vertical picture and enlarges it, so the horizontal video has the same width as the vertical one. ContentCam's Single Lens records the full 4:3 frame, so each format gets its full width or height at full resolution, in up to 4K at 60 fps. ContentCam's Dual Lens goes further: one rear lens films the vertical, another films the horizontal, each framed on its own.
  • 4K and 60 fps. ContentCam records 4K in all three modes: Single Lens at up to 60 fps, Dual Lens in vertical 4K, and Front & Back with both cameras, on supported iPhones. DualCam's settings say "4K is available in single-camera mode only", and 60 fps didn't work in any mode: with 4K and 60 fps selected, the file it saved was 1920 x 1080 at 29.99 fps.
  • Free version. DualCam gives you the first 60 seconds of recording free, then the video stops and the paywall opens. ContentCam's free version records unlimited Single Lens videos in portrait and landscape at once, in 1080p, plus 5 Dual Lens and 5 Front & Back recordings. Neither app has ads or a watermark.
  • Camera controls. DualCam's only manual control is an exposure slider with no reset. Tap to focus shows a yellow square but didn't focus, and AE/AF lock sits in a menu two taps away instead of on tap and hold. ContentCam has manual exposure, white balance, ISO and shutter, tap to focus, AE/AF lock on tap and hold, a zoom dial and a teleprompter.
  • Your photo library. DualCam asks for full access to your photos, although its privacy policy says it doesn't access or read your existing library. ContentCam only asks to add videos, so it can't see your library.
  • Platforms. ContentCam is on iPhone and Android. DualCam is iPhone only.

What did I find when I tested DualCam 1.0.5?

On 12 November 2026 I installed DualCam 1.0.5 from the App Store and tried every mode and setting in the free version until the paywall stopped me. This is what I found.

  • 60 seconds free, then pay. The paywall says "First 60 seconds free". When a take reaches one minute, recording stops and the app asks you to subscribe. Plans are weekly, yearly (US$22.99 a year with a 3-day trial, more than ContentCam's yearly plan, which has a 7-day trial) or lifetime.
  • No 4K with both cameras. The settings screen says "4K is available in single-camera mode only". Front & back recording is HD.
  • No 60 fps. You can select 4K and 60 fps, but the file I saved was 1080p at 29.99 fps. In dual-format mode the app shows "60 fps unavailable". I didn't get 60 fps in single-camera, dual-format or front & back mode.
  • The 16:9 file is a crop of the vertical one. In dual-format mode the preview draws a dashed 16:9 box inside the vertical picture, and that box is enlarged to make the 1920 x 1080 horizontal file. The horizontal video shows no more width than the vertical video, and in my test the preview's box didn't line up exactly with what was recorded.
  • Picture-in-picture can't be resized. Pinching does nothing and there's no size button.
  • Few camera controls. Exposure is the only manual control, and there's no reset button, so you drag it back to 0 by hand. Tap to focus shows a yellow square but didn't change focus, AE/AF lock isn't on tap and hold but in a menu two taps away, where it's easy to switch off by accident, and there are no zoom buttons or zoom dial on the preview.
  • Stabilisation didn't seem to work. With Video Stabilization on, my handheld clips still looked unstabilised. A 1-star US review from 8 August says "there's no stabilization".
  • Recordings lost. When I force-closed the app, went to the home screen or locked the phone mid-take, the recording was lost. ContentCam keeps recording through auto-lock and restores a take after a force-close the next time you open it.
  • File names that don't match. The vertical and horizontal files from the same take get different random names, and the only clue is "_portrait" or "_landscape" at the end. ContentCam saves into projects, scenes and takes with matching file names.
  • Full photo library access. DualCam asks for full access to your Photo Library "to save your recordings and show your recent videos", and adds its own DualCam album to Photos. Full access lets an app see every photo and video you have, yet its privacy policy says "We do not access, read, or transmit any existing content in your photo library." Saving a video doesn't need full access. ContentCam only asks to add videos to Photos, so it can't see your library.
  • An account you didn't make. Settings has "Delete account and all data", but the app never asked me to sign up or mentioned an account. Its privacy policy explains that it creates an anonymous account for each install, and sends crash reports to Sentry, including your IP address and a short log of recent in-app actions. ContentCam has no account.
  • Missing features. No teleprompter, no Dual Lens mode (two rear lenses at once), no 25 or 50 fps, no timelapse, no Apple Log, no audio level meter, and no projects or take management. ContentCam has all of these.

Does DualCam record 4K with both cameras?

No. DualCam's own settings say "4K is available in single-camera mode only", and in my test on 12 November 2026 front and back recording was HD at 30 fps. Its listing says "Up to 4K and up to 60 fps, depending on device and mode", but with 4K and 60 fps selected the file I saved was 1920 x 1080 at 29.99 fps. ContentCam's Front & Back records both cameras in 4K on supported iPhones (from iOS 2.9), as two separate files. FAQ

Is DualCam free?

Only for the first 60 seconds of recording. In my test the recording stopped at one minute and the app asked me to subscribe (weekly, yearly or lifetime). ContentCam's free version has no time limit: unlimited Single Lens recording in 1080p, 5 Dual Lens and 5 Front & Back takes, with no ads and no watermark.

Is DualCam on Android?

No. I found no Android version from DualCam's developer, Mobixoft. The App Store listing is for iPhone and requires iOS 18. ContentCam is on iPhone and Android.

Does DualCam save the front and back camera as separate files?

DualCam's listing describes showing yourself and the scene in one video with picture-in-picture or split screen, and does not mention separate front and back files. ContentCam's Front & Back mode saves the selfie camera and the rear camera as two separate files, and its Combine editor can render a picture-in-picture or split-screen video afterwards, keeping the originals.

Can DualCam record vertical and horizontal video at the same time?

ContentCam's Single Lens records the camera's full 4:3 frame and cuts both from it, so the horizontal keeps the full width and the vertical keeps the full height, in HD or 4K at up to 60 fps. Its Dual Lens mode films the vertical and the horizontal through two different lenses.

Does DualCam record 60 fps?
