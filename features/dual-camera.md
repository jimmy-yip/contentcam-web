# Dual Camera Video Recording on iPhone - ContentCam

Source: https://contentcam.app/features/dual-camera/

How ContentCam records portrait and landscape simultaneously: Single Lens full-FOV capture from the 4:3 sensor, or Dual Lens with wide + ultra-wide at once.

ContentCam records 9:16 portrait and 16:9 landscape video simultaneously. There are two ways it does this, and the difference matters more than it sounds.

## The problem with the obvious approach

The obvious way to get two formats is to record portrait, then crop a 16:9 strip out of the middle. That works, and it's what most apps in this category do. The cost: your "landscape" video is a zoomed-in slice of the portrait frame. Everything to the left and right of the phone-shaped view is gone, because the sensor never captured it.

## Single Lens mode: crop from the full sensor

Your phone's camera sensor is 4:3, wider than portrait and taller than landscape. ContentCam records at that native 4:3, then cuts both deliverables out of the bigger frame.

### Portrait: full height

The 9:16 crop uses the sensor's full vertical field of view.

### Landscape: full width

The 16:9 crop uses the sensor's full horizontal field of view. Not a strip of the portrait, an actual wide shot.

Works on every supported phone, front camera included, and it's the mode 4K uses. This is the free tier's recording mode.

## Dual Lens mode: two cameras, two framings

On iPhone 11 and later, ContentCam runs Apple's multi-camera capture: the wide lens records your portrait while the ultra-wide records landscape, at the same instant.

The point is genuinely different framing. Your portrait can be a tight shot for Reels while the landscape is a wide establishing shot for YouTube, from one press of the button. You get 5 free Dual Lens takes to try it; unlimited with [Premium](https://contentcam.app/pricing/). iPhone-only for now.

## Common questions

### Is this the same as front + back recording?

They're different modes, and ContentCam has both. Dual Lens (this page) records two rear cameras for two framings of the same scene. **Front & Back mode** records the front and rear cameras at once (your reaction and your POV in a single take) for reaction-style content.

### Do both files record audio?

Yes, both files carry the same 48kHz AAC audio track.

### Does Dual Lens drain the battery faster?

Running two cameras costs more power than one, yes. For long sessions, Single Lens is the more efficient choice.
