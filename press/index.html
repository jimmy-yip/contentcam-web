In one line

ContentCam records portrait (9:16) and landscape (16:9) video at the same time, so one take gives creators a vertical video for TikTok, Reels and Shorts and a horizontal one for YouTube.

The facts

What it isA camera app for iPhone and Android that records 9:16 and 16:9 video in one take, as two separate files. Both use the camera's full frame.
Who makes itJimmy Yip, a solo independent developer in Sydney, Australia.
PlatformsiPhone (iPhone XS and later, iOS 18+), listed on the App Store as "ContentCam: Dual Camera Video". Android 8.0 and later on Google Play.
LaunchedApp Store, May 2026. Google Play, 8 June 2026.
Recording modesSingle Lens (full sensor, both formats), Dual Lens (two rear cameras at once on iPhone), Front & Back (your reaction and the scene at once, as two files). From iOS 2.9, Dual Lens and Front & Back record in 4K on supported iPhones.

The story

Jimmy built ContentCam for his girlfriend, who is a creator. He kept watching her film every scene twice: once vertical for Reels and TikTok, once horizontal for YouTube. Cropping one out of the other doesn't fix it, because a horizontal strip cut from a vertical video loses the sides of the scene and most of the resolution.

So ContentCam reads the camera's full 4:3 frame and cuts both videos from it. The landscape file keeps the full width of the scene and the portrait keeps its full height, from a single tap.

"I kept watching her film every scene twice, and figured one take should give you both."Jimmy Yip, developer

What sets it apart

Full frame in both formats

Most apps record vertical and cut a horizontal strip out of the middle for YouTube. ContentCam frames each format from the camera's full sensor.

4K with two cameras

Since iOS 2.9, Dual Lens and Front & Back record in 4K on supported iPhones. Very few camera apps do 4K with two cameras running at once.

Front & Back on more phones

Apple's Dual Capture is limited to recent iPhones and saves one picture-in-picture file. ContentCam's Front & Back works on any iPhone running iOS 18 and on supported Android phones, as two separate files.

Built by one person

A solo developer who reads every review and feature request, and ships an update every week or two.
