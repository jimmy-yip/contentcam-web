## What is recording front and back at the same time good for?

A few formats live or die on having both cameras at once:

- **Reactions.** Your face over the clip or message you're reacting to, with real timing.
- **Unboxings and demos.** Your hands on the product from the rear camera, your face reacting from the front. One take, no cutting between two clips.
- **POV with commentary.** Show the view, the meal, the workout, with you in the corner talking through it as it happens.
- **Tutorials.** The rear camera on the task, your face explaining it.

The two shots have to be simultaneous. A reaction filmed after the fact reads as fake, because it is.

## How does front-and-back recording work on iPhone?

Recent iPhones can run more than one camera at the same time. A front-and-back mode uses that: it records your rear lens and your front lens together and puts your face into the frame, usually as a smaller inset over the main shot. You press record once and both cameras roll on the same timeline, so audio and reactions stay in sync.

Recording two *rear* cameras for two framings of one scene is dual-lens, a [different feature](https://contentcam.app/features/dual-camera/).

## How do you shoot front and back video well?

The front camera is the weaker sensor and the audio is easy to get wrong, so a few things make a real difference:

- **Light your face.** Sit facing a window or a lamp. The selfie camera gets noisy in dim rooms faster than the rear one does, and your face is the part people watch.
- **Mind the inset.** Frame the rear shot with one corner left clear for your face.
- **Talk toward the phone.** Phone mics favour whoever's closest and loudest. If you're a step back showing something, your voice drops. Keep it up, or come closer for the lines that matter.
- **Lock exposure if you move.** Walking from a bright window to a dark hallway makes both cameras hunt. ContentCam's [manual controls](https://contentcam.app/features/manual-controls/) hold it steady.
