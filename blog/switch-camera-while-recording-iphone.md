# How to Switch Camera While Recording on iPhone (2026)

Source: https://contentcam.app/blog/switch-camera-while-recording-iphone/

The iPhone Camera app can't flip between the front and back camera mid-recording. Three ways around it: stop and flip, Dual Capture on iPhone 17, or record both cameras as two files and cut between them.

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 3 November 2026 · Updated 12 November 2026

You can't switch between the front and back camera mid-recording in the iPhone's own Camera app; Apple's iPhone User Guide lists what you can do while recording, and flipping the camera isn't on it. The cleanest way around it is to record both cameras at once as two separate files and cut between them in the edit, which is what ContentCam's Front & Back mode does (I build ContentCam). It works on every iPhone that runs iOS 18, including iPhone 15 and 16, which don't have Apple's Dual Capture, with no ads and no watermark. The other options: stop and flip between clips, or use Dual Capture on iPhone 17 or later, which saves one video with the front camera fixed in a window.

## Can you switch camera while recording on iPhone?

Not in Apple's Camera app. Apple's page [Record a video with your iPhone camera](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/record-videos-iph61f49e4bb/ios) (iOS 27 edition, checked 5 November 2026) says that while recording you can take a still photo, tap Pause, pinch to zoom, or tap the .5x, 1x, 2x and other zoom buttons. Those buttons only change rear lenses, and the guide describes no control that swaps to the front camera during a take.

That holds for every model from iPhone 11 to iPhone 18 Pro, because it's a Camera app limit, not a hardware one. If your iPhone is jumping between rear lenses on its own, that's a different problem: [why the iPhone camera switches lenses while recording](https://contentcam.app/blog/iphone-camera-switching-lenses-while-recording/).

## Why can't you switch camera while recording on iPhone?

Because a recording is one continuous file from one camera. Swapping means stopping one sensor and starting another, with a different field of view and colour, inside the same file. Apple's Camera app doesn't attempt it, and apps that flip mid-take show a brief jump.

## How do you switch between front and back camera in the edit instead?

Record both cameras into separate files, then cut between them like a two-camera TV shoot: the view, then your face at the exact moment you reacted, then the view again.

1. **Record front and back at the same time** in ContentCam's Front & Back mode, which saves each camera as its own file. Apple has let third-party apps run two cameras at once since iOS 13.
2. **Clap once at the start.** The spike in the audio waveform is your sync point.
3. **Stack the two clips** in iMovie or CapCut, rear camera on the main track, front camera above it, lined up on the clap.
4. **Cut the top layer** wherever you want your face to show. Every cut is a "camera switch", and you can move the cuts later.

Front & Back comes with 5 free recordings, then Premium. On iPhone, ContentCam's Combine editor can also turn a take into one picture-in-picture or split-screen video with the two original files kept, so you can post the combined version and still cut between angles. No account, and the footage stays on your phone. From iOS 2.9 it records in 4K on supported iPhones, and at up to 60 fps on iPhones that support it (60 fps is Premium).

## How does Dual Capture work instead of switching?

Dual Capture records the front camera and the back camera together in Apple's Camera app: open Camera, choose Video, tap the camera-controls button, then tap Dual Capture, per the same Apple guide. Apple's iPhone 17 and iPhone Air specs pages list it at up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps.

The catch is the layout. Apple's guide illustration shows the front camera in a window at the top left over the rear view, and everything Apple publishes points to one file with that window painted in. You can't cut to a clean rear-only shot later, and iPhone 16, 16e and 17e don't have Dual Capture at all. More in [which iPhones have Dual Capture, and where it stops](https://contentcam.app/blog/iphone-17-dual-capture-limitations/).

## How do you switch cameras by stopping and starting?

Record the first part on the back camera and stop, tap the flip button (the circular arrows, bottom right), record the next part, then join the clips end to end in iMovie or CapCut. It's free on every iPhone. The limit: nothing in clip one happens at the same moment as clip two, so for a reaction or an unboxing, the face filmed after the event reads as acted.

## Which apps let you flip the camera mid-recording?

Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok have their own flip controls, and mid-take behaviour changes between app versions, so test before you rely on it. In Snapchat's Dual Camera, Snapchat Support says the flip button swaps which camera is the primary view. Either way you get one file with the switch baked in.
