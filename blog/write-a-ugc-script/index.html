How Do You Write a UGC Script That Doesn't Sound Scripted? (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 21 October 2026

A UGC script sounds scripted for three measurable reasons: it has too many words for the running time, its sentences are longer than anyone speaks, and it carries a brand's adjectives instead of the creator's own nouns. Write to the clock at about 2.5 words a second, cap sentences at 15 words, and say the product's name twice at most. The delivery problem that creators try to fix with practice is usually a word count problem on the page.

How long should a UGC script be in words?

Count on 2.5 words a second, which is 150 words a minute. Read a paragraph of your own writing aloud at a natural pace with a stopwatch running and the result will land close to that. On camera the pace drops, so write to the low end of the count rather than the high end.

Leave about one second of silence at the head of the script and one at the tail. An editor needs that handle, and a word spoken in the first half second of a clip is a word nobody hears. Subtract those two seconds before counting: a 30 second brief is a 28 second script, so 70 words rather than 75.

What structure does a 30 second UGC script follow?

Five beats, with the seconds allocated before a single line is written. The allocation below is for a 30 second clip, and every number scales.

  • Hook, 0 to 2 seconds, 6 words. A concrete statement with a noun in it, no greeting.
  • Problem, 2 to 7 seconds, 12 words. The specific annoyance, in the words a friend would use.
  • The moment of use, 7 to 18 seconds, 25 words. You, using the product, on camera, while talking.
  • Proof, 18 to 25 seconds, 18 words. One detail only you could know, from a week of actually using the thing.
  • Close, 25 to 28 seconds, 9 words. What the viewer does next, said once.

Show the product in use before explaining what the product does. A script that explains for 12 seconds and demonstrates for 4 is a voiceover with footage attached, and a brand can tell the difference immediately.

How do you write a hook for the first two seconds?

Give the hook 6 to 8 words and start it on a concrete noun. Two seconds at 2.5 words a second is five words, so a hook that opens with "Hey guys, so today I wanted to talk about" has spent the entire hook on nothing. Compare "This is the third one I've broken" against "I want to tell you about a product I've been loving lately". The first is 7 words and names a situation. The second is 16 words and names nothing.

Write three hooks for every script. The strongest of the three is rarely the first one written, and having all three on the page saves a reshoot when a brand asks for variants. Film them against the same second beat so the versions cut together from one session.

Why do UGC scripts sound scripted?

Six tells, in the order they show up in drafts:

  • Adjective stacking. Three adjectives in front of a noun is marketing copy, not speech. Cut to zero adjectives and let the shot carry the quality.
  • Sentences over 15 words. A long sentence forces a read-ahead, and the eye movement is visible on camera.
  • No contractions. "It is" and "you will" read as written text. "It's" and "you'll" read as speech.
  • Feature lists. Four features in 30 seconds means 7 seconds each, which is not long enough to land any of them.
  • The brand name four times. Twice is the ceiling: once in the middle beat, once in the close.
  • Explaining what the frame already shows. If the shot shows the lid clicking shut, the line about the lid clicking shut is a duplicate.

The repair is mechanical. Read the draft aloud, note every point where you stumble, and rewrite those lines only. A stumble marks a sentence you would never have said. Then cut the whole script by a third and check the word count against the table above.

How do you include a brand's talking points without sounding like an ad?

Carry one claim per script, placed in the middle beat, rewritten in your own words and attached to something you physically do on camera. A brief that lists five selling points is a brief that has not been edited yet. Ask the brand which single point the campaign is being measured on, and build the middle beat on that one.

Keep the mandated elements separate from the claim: the product name, the price or offer if there is one, the call to action, and the paid partnership disclosure that the platform and your local regulator require. Those are fixed text. Put them in the close, where fixed text sounds like a sign-off instead of an interruption, and keep the first 18 seconds entirely in your own vocabulary.

How do you script the shots and not just the words?

Write the script in two columns: the spoken line on the left, the shot on the right. A 30 second UGC clip runs 6 to 8 shots at 3 to 5 seconds each, and deciding those shots at the desk is what stops a shoot becoming 40 minutes of deciding where to point the phone.

Name the shot in concrete terms on the right: "hands only, product on bench, phone at 40cm" rather than "product shot". Mark which lines are spoken on camera and which are voiceover over b-roll, because voiceover lines can be rewritten after the shoot and on-camera lines cannot.

How do you script one take that works vertical and wide?

Keep every action the script depends on inside the centre third of the frame. Cropping a 16:9 recording to a full height 9:16 vertical keeps 81/256 of the original width, which is 31.6%, so any gesture, product or caption written into the left or right of a wide frame is absent from the vertical cut. A line like "and look what's on the shelf behind me" is a line that only works in one aspect ratio.

Write one script, mark the two or three lines whose visual lands at the frame edge, and move those visuals to the centre before filming rather than after. Common questions

Should you memorise a UGC script or read it from a prompter? Memorise the five beats and the hook word for word, then let the middle vary between takes. Word-perfect recall of 75 words is what produces the recited delivery, and a prompter solves the memory problem while creating an eyeline problem. Reading a script without looking like you are reading covers the prompter setup.

How many takes should you film per UGC script? Three complete takes, then the two alternate hooks on their own. Three is enough to have one usable version of every beat, and a fourth complete take is usually worse than the second because energy drops faster than accuracy improves.

Can you improvise a UGC video instead of scripting it? Write the hook and the close, improvise the middle. The hook and the close are the two places where a wasted second costs the most, and they are the two shortest pieces of writing in the job.

Do brands want to see the UGC script before you film? Send the script and the three hook variants for approval before the shoot. Changing a line in a document takes a minute and changing it after delivery means filming the whole thing again.
