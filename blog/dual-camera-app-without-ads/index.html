"None declared" means the App Store listing has no advertising line under Age Rating and the description doesn't mention ads. That is weaker evidence than an app saying "no ads" outright. More apps, ads aside, are compared in the best front and back camera apps.

Why does an ad in a camera app cost you the shot?

A full-screen ad covers the viewfinder and the record button. A reaction, a goal or a sunset happens once, and while an ad is on screen you can't frame or start the take. Front and back recording is hit hardest, because the shot is usually your face reacting to something live. PairTake's own Play listing makes the same point: with no third-party ads, "an interstitial does not stand between you and the shutter".

How do you tell if a camera app has ads before installing it?

  1. Google Play: look for "Contains ads" under the app name. Google's Play Console Help says "Apps that contain ads show a 'Contains ads' label on their store listing", and Google may add the label itself if a developer declares wrongly.
  2. App Store: scroll to Information, then Age Rating. Apple asks developers to disclose features such as advertising there, and apps that answer yes show "Contains Advertising" under the rating. Dualgram, MixCam - Dual Video Recorder and Dual Camera: Shot Front & Back all show the line.
  3. In-app purchases: on either store, a purchase named "Remove Ads" means the free version shows ads. "Remove Watermark" means free exports carry the app's logo.
  4. Privacy label: "Advertising Data" or "Third-Party Advertising" on an App Store privacy label is a weaker signal. Those categories cover in-app ads, but also apps that only measure their own marketing elsewhere, so check the Age Rating line first.
