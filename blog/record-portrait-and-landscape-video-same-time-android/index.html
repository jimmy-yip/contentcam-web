How to Record Portrait and Landscape Video at the Same Time on Android (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 30 September 2026

The short answer: the camera app that came with your Android phone can't do it. Samsung Camera and Pixel Camera record in one shape per take. To get a 9:16 vertical file and a 16:9 horizontal file from the same take, you either crop one out of the other afterwards, shoot in 4K so the crop has pixels to spare, or use an app that records both files at once from the full sensor.

I build one of those apps, so read the maths below as the useful part. The arithmetic is the same whichever tool you pick.

Can the stock Android camera record portrait and landscape at once?

No. The stock Android camera apps record in one shape per take: the one you picked, or the one you held the phone in.

  • Samsung Camera has an aspect ratio setting in the camera, and Samsung's own support page lists 9:16, 1:1 and Full among the options, with the caveat that "available ratios may vary depending on your device's camera specifications." You choose one. The phone records one.
  • Pixel Camera: Google's help page for the Pixel Camera app documents aspect ratio choices for photos (Wide crop 16:9 and Full image 4:3). It documents no aspect ratio choice for video. A Pixel video is horizontal or vertical depending on how you hold the phone.
  • Google Photos can crop a finished video to a different aspect ratio afterwards. That is Option 1 below, with the same cost.

Neither Samsung's nor Google's support pages describe a mode that saves a portrait file and a landscape file from one take.

Option 1: How much do you lose cropping landscape video to portrait?

You keep 31.6% of the width and throw away 68.4% of the scene. Here is the arithmetic for a standard 1080p landscape video:

  • The landscape frame is 1920×1080.
  • A 9:16 crop at full height is 1080 × 9 ÷ 16 = 607.5 pixels wide.
  • 607.5 ÷ 1920 = 0.316, so 31.6% of the width survives.

Then the 607×1080 strip gets scaled up to 1080×1920 for TikTok, Reels or Shorts. That is 1.78 times on each axis, which is why cropped verticals look soft. Going the other way is worse: a 16:9 crop of a 1080×1920 vertical is a 1080×607 strip stretched to 1920×1080, from a slice that only ever saw a phone-shaped view of the room.

Crop in post when the person fills the middle of the frame and the second format is a bonus. Avoid it for travel, sport, product shots, or any scene where the background matters.

Option 2: Can you shoot 4K landscape and reframe it as vertical?

Yes, and on a phone that records 4K this is the best free fix. A 9:16 crop from a 3840×2160 frame is 2160 × 9 ÷ 16 = 1215×2160 pixels, which is more than the 1080×1920 a vertical platform plays. The resolution problem from Option 1 goes away.

The framing problem stays. The vertical is still 31.6% of the landscape width, so the subject must sit dead centre for the whole take, and anything you composed for the wide shot (a skyline, a second person, a product on the bench) is outside the vertical. You also edit every video twice, and 4K has four times the pixels of 1080p, so the files fill your phone much faster.

Option 3: How do you record both formats at once on Android?

Record the sensor's full 4:3 frame and cut both shapes out of it during capture. A phone sensor is 4:3, which is wider than a 9:16 frame and taller than a 16:9 frame. Take an illustrative 4000×3000 sensor frame:

MethodVertical slice of a 4000×3000 sensorShare of sensor area
Record 16:9, crop 9:16 laterabout 1266×225024%
Crop 9:16 from the full 4:3 frameabout 1688×300042%

The full-frame vertical sees 1.78 times more of the scene, and the 16:9 landscape keeps the sensor's full width (4000×2250). Neither file is a crop of the other.
