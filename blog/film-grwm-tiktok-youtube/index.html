How Do You Film a GRWM Video for TikTok and YouTube at Once? (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 30 October 2026

Film the get-ready sequence once, framed so that both a 9:16 vertical crop and a 16:9 wide crop sit inside the same picture, with white balance and exposure locked before the first brush goes on. A get-ready-with-me video is the one format where a second take is impossible, because foundation, hair and a finished outfit cannot be undone.

Why can't you just crop a GRWM video from vertical to wide?

Because a 16:9 crop taken from a 1080 × 1920 vertical file keeps only 1080 × 608 pixels, which is 31.6% of the vertical frame and throws away 68% of it. The arithmetic is fixed: a 16:9 rectangle at 1080 px wide is 1080 × 9 ÷ 16 = 607.5 px tall. A YouTube version cut that way is a letterboxed strip of your face with the mirror, the outfit and the dressing table gone.

A 4:3 sensor frame solves the problem because 4:3 is taller than 16:9 and wider than 9:16 at the same time. Take a 4032 × 3024 capture. The tallest 9:16 column it contains is 1701 × 3024, and the widest 16:9 band it contains is 4032 × 2268. Both of those exceed 1080p, so both crops downscale to full-resolution deliverables from one recording. That is the whole reason to shoot a GRWM in 4:3 rather than in the format you intend to post.

Where should the phone sit for a GRWM in a bathroom or bedroom?

Put the lens at your standing eye height, roughly 1.5 to 1.65 m for most adults, and 1.5 to 2 m back for a frame that holds you from the waist up. A phone propped on a vanity at 1 m looks up your nose and puts the ceiling light in shot. Eye height is the single correction that does the most for a get-ready video.

Then keep every action inside the central vertical column, because that column is all the TikTok cut will contain. Leaning sideways to reach a hairbrush takes your head out of the 9:16 crop while staying comfortably inside the 16:9 one, and you will not notice until you are editing.

How do you stop bathroom lights banding in a GRWM video?

Match the shutter speed to the mains frequency, not to the 180 degree rule. Mains-powered LED and fluorescent lamps ripple in brightness at twice the mains frequency: 100 Hz on the 50 Hz supply used in Australia, the United Kingdom and most of Europe, and 120 Hz on the 60 Hz supply used in the United States, Canada and parts of Japan. Rolling bands appear when the exposure time is not a whole number of flicker cycles.

  • 50 Hz mains: use 1/50 s (20 ms, two cycles) or 1/100 s (10 ms, one cycle).
  • 60 Hz mains: use 1/60 s (16.7 ms, two cycles) or 1/120 s (8.3 ms, one cycle).

Note what that means in Australia. The 180 degree rule asks for 1/60 s at 30fps, and 1/60 s is 16.7 ms, which is not a whole number of 10 ms cycles. 1/60 s is the shutter that bands on 50 Hz lighting. Shoot 30fps at 1/50 s instead and accept a touch more motion blur, or 1/100 s if the room is bright enough. High-frequency LED drivers do not flicker at all, so test your own vanity lights with a 10 second recording before you commit to a long sequence.

What white balance should a GRWM be locked to?

Pick one colour temperature, lock it, and remove whichever light source disagrees. Warm white domestic bulbs sit around 2700 to 3000K, neutral white fittings around 4000K, direct daylight around 5600K, and open shade or an overcast sky at 6500 to 7500K. A bathroom with a window and a warm vanity globe is two sources at once, and automatic white balance will shift your skin tone every time you lean toward the mirror.

So close the blind and shoot at 3000K, or switch the globe off and shoot at 5600K. Lock exposure as well: auto exposure rides up the moment you hold a dark mascara tube into the frame, and the brightness pump is visible in both cuts. Locked focus matters too, because leaning in to do eyeliner at 25 cm will make a camera that is still hunting rack between your face and the mirror behind you.

How do you get usable audio in a tiled bathroom?

Get the microphone within 20 to 30 cm of your mouth, or record the narration separately afterwards. Sound follows the inverse square law, so every doubling of distance between mouth and microphone drops the level by about 6 dB, roughly a quarter of the sound power. A phone 2 m away on a vanity sits 8 times further from your mouth than a lavalier clipped at 25 cm, and hard-tiled reflections fill the gap.

So clip a lavalier microphone below the collarbone, or film the sequence without usable sound and record a voiceover later in a carpeted room. Turn the extraction fan off before you start, because fan rumble and a running tap are broadband noise sitting on top of speech and no editor strips either cleanly.

What does the mirror reverse, and what breaks because of it?

A mirror reverses text, so every product label, logo and printed slogan reads backwards in a mirror shot. Hold the serum bottle toward the lens rather than toward the mirror when you name it. Do not fix it by flipping the footage, because a horizontal flip also moves your hair parting, your watch and any asymmetry in your face to the wrong side, and people who know you will see that something is off without being able to say what.

How do you cut one GRWM take into a TikTok version and a YouTube version?

Treat the two cuts as different edits of identical footage. My own split: 30 to 60 seconds for the vertical cut, 8 to 12 cuts in it, the hook in the first 2 seconds, and the finished-look reveal held for the last 3 seconds. The wide cut runs 5 to 8 minutes and keeps what the short version cannot carry, which is the talking, the product reasoning and the step that went wrong.

Recording both frames at the same moment is the awkward part with a stock camera app, and the problem ContentCam exists to solve: one take, a 9:16 file and a 16:9 file written at once. Two phones side by side also work if you can live with matching their colour afterwards.

Common questions

Do you need two phones to film a GRWM for TikTok and YouTube? No. A single 4:3 capture at 4032 × 3024 already contains a 1701 × 3024 vertical crop and a 4032 × 2268 wide crop, so one camera can supply both deliverables. Two phones are only necessary if you want two genuinely different angles, such as a wide of the room plus a tight shot of the makeup.

What frame rate should a GRWM be filmed at? 30fps, with the shutter matched to your mains frequency. Choose 60fps only if you plan to slow the reveal or a hair flick down, and remember that 60fps roughly doubles the data rate at the same visual quality, which matters when one session is already writing two files.

Should a GRWM be filmed in the bathroom at all? The bathroom has the mirror and also the worst acoustics, the hardest surfaces and usually the worst light in the house. A bedroom with a mirror leaned against the wall beside a window gives softer light, less reverberation and more room to place the phone 2 m back.

Can mixed lighting be corrected after filming? Partly. One wrong but constant colour temperature can be corrected globally in a few seconds. A take where automatic white balance drifted between a window and a warm globe needs keyframed correction across the whole clip, which is why locking the value before recording is cheaper than fixing it later.
