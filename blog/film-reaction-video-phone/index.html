How to Film a Reaction Video on Your Phone

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 10 September 2026

Every guide to filming a reaction video assumes you are sitting at a desk with a capture card. You are holding the only device you need, and it can do this four different ways. Three of them are free and already installed.

How do you film a reaction video on a phone?

Record your face and the thing you are reacting to at the same time, in one take. That is the whole job, and there are four routes to it.

  • The in-app effect. TikTok's Dual effect, Snapchat's Dual Camera and Instagram's dual capture all record front and back together. Free, instant, and the output is a finished post.
  • Dual Capture in the iPhone 17 Camera app. Apple added it across the iPhone 17 line. It records up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30fps with a movable picture-in-picture. (Reported by MacRumors and Tom's Guide, checked 10 September 2026.)
  • Screen recording plus your face. If you are reacting to something on the phone itself, screen record and film yourself separately, then stack them in the edit.
  • A dedicated app. Worth it only when you need the two angles as usable footage rather than one baked file.

Start with the free route. If it does what you need, stop there.

Where should the small frame go?

Put your face in a corner, and keep it out of the bottom third.

Every vertical platform layers its own interface over the frame. The bottom of a TikTok or a Reel carries the caption, the username and a row of buttons, and the right edge carries the action column. A face parked in the bottom right is the one position guaranteed to be covered on at least one platform.

Top left is the safest corner. Keep the face frame large enough to read an expression, which in practice means at least a fifth of the frame width. Smaller than that and the reaction stops being legible, which defeats the point.

How do you fix the audio problem?

This is the part almost nobody covers, and it is the part that ruins most reaction videos.

You are reacting to something that makes sound, while making sound yourself. If both play out loud into one microphone, they compete, and your phone's automatic level control will duck one of them unpredictably.

Three fixes, in order of how well they work:

  • Wear headphones and add the source audio in the edit. Your microphone hears only you, cleanly, and you lay the original audio underneath afterwards at whatever level you want. This is the real answer.
  • Screen record, which captures the source audio on its own track. Then your voice stays separate by default.
  • If both must play into one mic, put the source speaker well behind you and keep your own mic within about 30cm of your mouth. The distance ratio does the separating.

Whatever you choose, turn off any automatic gain control the app offers. It is what makes reaction audio pump up and down.

How do you make a reaction look natural?

React late rather than early, and do not narrate.

The common failure is performing the reaction before the moment lands, because you already know what is coming. Watching something genuinely for the first time on camera is worth more than any amount of editing. If you have already seen it, react to a detail you missed rather than faking surprise at the main beat.

Leave the silences in. A pause with a changing expression holds attention better than filling the gap with commentary, and it gives you clean cut points.

What if you want the two angles as separate files?

Then the in-app effects will not do, because they hand you one file with the layout baked in. You cannot move the small frame afterwards, resize it, or cut to one angle on its own.

That matters if the reaction is going to be edited properly, or if the same take has to go to a vertical platform and a widescreen one. Separate files are the difference between footage and a finished post.

Common questions

Can you film a reaction video with just one phone? Yes. Front and back record together on most modern phones, either through the app you are posting to or through the Camera app on an iPhone 17.

Do you need two phones? No, but a second phone is useful as a dedicated audio recorder or as a screen to react to.

How do you stop your voice and the video audio fighting? Wear headphones so only your voice reaches the microphone, then add the source audio in the edit.

Where should the face cam go in a vertical video? Top left. The bottom third and the right edge are covered by platform interface.

Why does my reaction video only work on one platform? Because it was captured in one aspect ratio, and a widescreen crop of a vertical frame keeps only about 32% of the height.
