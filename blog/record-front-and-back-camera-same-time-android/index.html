How to Record the Front and Back Camera at the Same Time on Android

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 8 October 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026

The most flexible way to record the front and back camera at once on Android is a camera app that saves your face and the scene as two separate files. ContentCam's Front & Back mode does that on any Android phone whose hardware can run both cameras together, including Pixels that support it, where Pixel Camera has no front and back mode. Some Samsung Galaxy phones also do it in the stock Camera app, but usually as one video with the layout fixed before you press record.

For iPhone and Instagram's built-in option, see the main guide to using the front and back camera at the same time, or iPhone Dual Capture for Apple's stock version.

Doing it in ContentCam on Android

Front & Back records the selfie and rear cameras together from one tap and gives you two files, not one baked-in layout. The rear camera records vertical or horizontal (you choose); the selfie is always vertical. You choose the layout afterwards: inset, split, cutaways, or two posts. For one finished video, the Combine editor renders the pair as picture-in-picture or split screen and keeps your originals.

  1. Install ContentCam from Google Play.
  2. Switch the mode to Front & Back. The app checks your phone's cameras and only offers the mode where it works.
  3. Choose vertical or horizontal for the rear camera.
  4. Frame the rear shot and record once. Both cameras share one timeline, so the reaction and the scene stay in sync.
  5. Trim and export the two clips, or open Combine for a single video.

Front & Back records HD at up to 30fps on Android and is Premium, with 5 free recordings to try it. Trying Combine layouts is free; saving one is Premium. The mode also takes front and back photo pairs (Premium). No ads and no watermark, free or Premium. The free tier records a 9:16 and a 16:9 file from one take, covered in recording portrait and landscape at the same time on Android.

Samsung Galaxy: Director's View and Dual recording

Samsung's support page describes Dual recording as a way to "record a video using the rear camera and selfie camera at the same time", on select devices running One UI 3.1 and later. You pick picture-in-picture (you in a corner) or split (rear on top, selfie below) before you record.

The limits, from Samsung's own pages: the result is normally one video with the inset baked in, so it can't be moved or removed later, and you can't change the Dual recording setting once a video has started. Only the Galaxy S22 and S23 series can save separate selfie and rear files, switched on before recording. On newer Galaxy phones, including the S24 and S25 series, Director's View has been renamed Dual recording, and from One UI 8.5 it sits in Video mode under Quick controls. Full steps are in how to record front and back on a Samsung Galaxy.

Google Pixel: no front and back mode

Pixel Camera doesn't record two cameras at once. Google's closest feature is Dual screen preview on the Pixel Fold models, which shows the viewfinder on the inside and outside screens so your subject can see themselves. It's still one camera recording. On a Pixel, front and back recording means a third-party app such as ContentCam.

Every other Android phone: it depends on the hardware

Android 11 (API level 30) added getConcurrentCameraIds(), which tells an app which cameras can run at the same time. If your phone reports a front and rear pair, an app can record both. If not, no app using that official route can. That's why it works on one phone and not another.

Which route should you use?

  • You want the two angles as separate files, or you're on a Pixel or another brand: ContentCam's Front & Back mode.
  • You have a supported Galaxy and want one finished clip fast: Dual recording in the stock Camera app.
  • You just want your face in a corner for one Story: Instagram's Dual camera, covered in the main dual camera guide.

Shooting it well

  • Light your face. The selfie camera gets noisy in a dim room first. Face a window or a lamp.
  • Keep your voice close. Step back to show something and your voice drops, so come closer for the lines that matter.
  • Lock exposure between light changes. The Android camera settings guide covers what your phone lets you lock.
  • Watch the heat. For long takes, keep out of direct sun and take the case off.

FAQ

Can a Google Pixel record the front and back camera at the same time?

Not in Pixel Camera. Dual screen preview on the Pixel Fold models shows the viewfinder on both screens while one camera records. On a Pixel that supports concurrent cameras, an app with a front and back mode, such as ContentCam, records both.

What is the best app to record the front and back camera at the same time on Android?

ContentCam, if you want to edit the two angles: Front & Back saves your face and the scene as two separate files on any Android phone that can run both cameras together, with no ads or watermark. On a supported Galaxy, stock Dual recording is the quickest way to one finished clip.

Does Samsung Dual recording save two separate files?

Usually not. It records one video with the layout you picked, and the setting can't be changed once recording starts. Samsung says only the Galaxy S22 and S23 series can save separate selfie and rear files, set before recording.

Is ContentCam's Front & Back mode free on Android?

ContentCam is free to download and its dual-format recording is free. Front & Back is a Premium mode with 5 free recordings to try it.

Why can't my Android phone record both cameras at once?

It comes down to the hardware. Android 11 added getConcurrentCameraIds(), which tells apps which cameras can run together. If a phone reports no front and rear pair, apps using that official route can't record both.
