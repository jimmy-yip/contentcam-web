How Do You Build a UGC Portfolio With No Clients? (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 28 October 2026

Film five spec pieces of 15 to 30 seconds each on five products already in your house, deliver each piece in four crops (1080×1920, 1080×1350, 1080×1080 and 1920×1080), and label every piece as unpaid spec work. Five pieces at 30 seconds is 2 minutes 30 of footage, which is about as much as any brand will watch. A UGC portfolio is a demonstration that you can light a face, record clean audio and hand over usable files on time. None of those three things requires a client, so an empty portfolio is a weekend of filming rather than a career problem.

What does a brand actually look at in a UGC portfolio?

A brand checks four things, roughly in this order: whether the face is lit, whether the audio is clean, whether the product is legible on a phone screen, and whether the files arrived in the sizes the brief asked for. Scripts, captions and personality come after those four. Assume the reviewer gives the whole portfolio about 90 seconds and watches the first 3 seconds of each piece, so put the strongest piece first and open every piece on a product in frame rather than a greeting.

The fourth item is the one creators skip, and the one that gets a second booking. Delivering a named set of files on a stated day is a signal about working with you, not about filming. A portfolio page that says "delivered 9:16, 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9, 3 business days" is making a promise a marketing manager recognises.

How many videos should a UGC portfolio have?

Five pieces, each 15 to 30 seconds, each a different format of UGC. Five is enough to show range and short enough to watch in one sitting. More than eight pieces means a reviewer judges you on the weakest one. Film the five below:

  • Testimonial. You on camera, talking to the lens, 25 to 30 seconds, 6 to 8 shots at 3 to 5 seconds each.
  • Hands-only demo. No face. Phone 40 cm above a bench, product centred, 15 to 20 seconds.
  • First use. Opening, assembling or applying the product, filmed in one continuous take of 20 to 30 seconds.
  • Problem and solution. 5 seconds of the annoyance, 15 seconds of the fix, 5 seconds of the result.
  • Voiceover over b-roll. Proves you can carry a brand's script over footage with no on-camera delivery at all.

Write each piece to about 2.5 words a second, so a 30 second clip carries 70 to 75 spoken words. Writing a UGC script that doesn't sound scripted has the full beat-by-beat word counts.

What should you film for a UGC portfolio when you have no clients?

Film products you already own and genuinely use, one from each of five different categories: skincare or grooming, kitchen or food, fitness, a tech accessory, and something household. Five categories beats five skincare pieces, because a brand hires on evidence that you can handle its category, and category variety is what lets five pieces cover twenty pitches.

Choose products you have used for at least a week. Every piece needs one specific detail that only a real user would say, and a product bought for the shoot cannot supply that detail. Keep the receipts and packaging out of frame, keep competitor products out of frame, and shoot a product whose label still looks new.

How do you label spec work so a brand is not misled?

Write "spec work, unpaid, not affiliated with the brand" as the first line of the portfolio page, and repeat the same words in the file name of every piece. Spec work is a normal and accepted way to build a reel in advertising, and the only thing that turns spec work into a problem is presenting spec work as a paid campaign.

Two things to avoid: adding a brand's logo, lower third or tagline in a way that implies the brand signed off, and posting spec pieces to a public feed with partnership wording attached. Keep spec pieces on the portfolio page and in the folder you send. Disclosure obligations for sponsored content differ by country and get rewritten regularly, so check your own regulator's current guidance rather than taking a filming blog's word for it.

What settings should you film UGC portfolio pieces at?

Film 1080p at 30 fps with focus, exposure and white balance locked before the take starts. 4K is worth it only when a brief asks for 4K or you plan to crop in; 1080p keeps file sizes small enough to send and is the resolution most short-form video ends up at anyway. The settings below are the whole list:

SettingValueWhy
Resolution1080p (4K only on request)Smaller files, faster delivery, no visible loss at phone size
Frame rate30 fps, or 60 fps for a slow-motion insert30 fps matches how most short-form video is watched
Shutter1/60 at 30 fps, 1/120 at 60 fpsDouble the frame rate gives natural motion blur
White balanceLocked: about 5600K in daylight, about 3200K under household bulbsAuto white balance shifts colour mid-take and the shift is unfixable in the edit
Exposure and focusLocked on the face, or on the product for hands-onlyStops the frame pumping brighter and darker when you move

Camera distance1.2 to 1.5 m at chest height for a testimonial, 40 cm overhead for handsKeeps the face out of wide-lens distortion and the product legible

Lock the three exposure settings on every piece in the portfolio and the five pieces will cut together as one body of work instead of five unrelated clips.

What files and sizes should a UGC portfolio deliver?

Deliver four crops of every piece: 9:16 at 1080×1920, 4:5 at 1080×1350, 1:1 at 1080×1080 and 16:9 at 1920×1080. Cutting those crops from one vertical recording is pure arithmetic, and the arithmetic decides what you can frame:

CropPixelsShare of a 1080×1920 vertical frame
9:161080×1920100%
4:51080×135070.3% of the height
1:11080×108056.25% of the height
16:91920×108031.6% of the height

The 16:9 row is why a wide version cut from a vertical file looks wrong: a 16:9 crop of a 1080×1920 frame keeps 607 of 1920 vertical pixels, so heads get clipped and a product held at waist height disappears. Either record the wide format in the same take or frame every action inside the middle 31.6% band. Name files so a marketing manager can find one piece six weeks later: yourname-product-spec-9x16-v1.mp4. Send a single folder link with the four crops of each piece, keep the original recordings for at least 12 months, and state a delivery window of 3 business days on the portfolio page.

How do you turn a UGC portfolio into a first paid job?

Pitch brands whose current ads you can describe a specific gap in, 10 brands a week, in 100 words with two links. Name the gap in the first sentence: a brand running only polished studio footage, or only 16:9 cutdowns on a vertical platform, gives you something concrete to say. A pitch that opens with "I love your brand" and attaches a portfolio is a pitch that is competing on volume alone.

Pick the one piece from the five that matches the brand's category and link that piece first, then the portfolio page. Rates sit outside what I can give you an honest figure for here, so ask the brand for the budget before quoting a number, and ask in writing what formats and how many revisions the budget covers.

Common questions

Do you need followers to get UGC work? No. UGC work is footage delivery, and the brand posts the footage on the brand's own account, so audience size is not the product being bought. A creator with 200 followers and five clean spec pieces is hireable; a creator with 50,000 followers and no deliverable files is not.

Can you put a brand's product in a portfolio without permission? Filming a product you bought, for a portfolio you show privately to prospective clients, labelled as unpaid spec work, is standard practice. Problems start when spec work implies endorsement or when a brand's logo is used as though the brand approved the piece. Trademark and advertising rules vary by country, so check your own jurisdiction before publishing spec work publicly.

How long should each UGC portfolio video be? 15 to 30 seconds, and 30 seconds is the ceiling. A 30 second piece at 2.5 words a second holds 70 to 75 words, which fits a hook, a demonstration and a close with nothing padding the middle.

Should a UGC portfolio be vertical only? No. Include at least one 16:9 version, because a brand that runs YouTube pre-roll or paid placements needs a wide file and most portfolios cannot supply one. Delivering four crops from one shoot is the cheapest point of difference available to a new creator.

How often should a UGC portfolio be updated? Replace the weakest of the five pieces every month and rotate in the category you are pitching most. Five pieces that stay current are more useful than fifteen that show every piece you have ever filmed.
