# How to Read a Script Without Looking Like You Are Reading (2026)

Source: https://contentcam.app/blog/read-script-without-looking-like-reading/

Write it the way you talk, put the text right under the lens, and vary your emphasis. Viewers spot a flat, even rhythm, not your eyes moving. The script and prompter fixes, step by step.

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 18 September 2026 · Updated 25 October 2026

To read a script without making it obvious, fix three things. Write it the way you talk: short sentences and contractions. Put the text right under the lens, so your eyes never drift off it. And mark the one word in each line you would stress in conversation. Viewers rarely catch you reading because your eyes move. They catch the flat, even rhythm of a sentence being recited.

Teleprompter advice splits between "just memorise it" and gear reviews. Neither addresses the actual tell. Viewers do not detect reading because your eyes move. They detect it because your delivery stops having a rhythm, and because you emphasise the wrong words. Both are fixable in how you write the script, before the prompter is involved at all.

## Why does reading look like reading?

Because written sentences and spoken sentences have different shapes, and reading a written one aloud produces a cadence nobody uses in conversation.

Three specific tells, in the order people notice them:

- **Even pacing.** Natural speech speeds up on familiar phrases and slows on the important word. Reading flattens everything to one tempo.
- **Wrong emphasis.** Reading puts stress on whatever sits at the end of a line, rather than on the word that carries the meaning.
- **Eye drift.** Real, but the smallest of the three, and the only one people try to fix.

## How should you write a script you intend to read?

Write it the way you talk, then cut it by a third.

Practical rules that change delivery more than any prompter setting:

- **Short sentences.** Under about fifteen words. Long ones force you to read ahead, which is when the eye movement becomes visible.
- **Contractions everywhere.** "You will" reads formal and sounds read. "You'll" sounds spoken.
- **One idea per line, with a line break after each.** The break is a breath mark, and it does most of the pacing work for you.
- **Read it aloud while writing.** Anything you stumble on is a sentence you would never have said.

Mark the word you want to stress in each sentence. Bold it, capitalise it, whatever your prompter shows. Knowing where the emphasis goes fixes the second tell entirely.

## How should the prompter be set up?

Text directly under the lens, a narrow column, and scrolling slightly slower than you speak.

Narrow matters more than people expect. A wide column makes your eyes track visibly left to right; a column a few words wide keeps the movement small enough to read as thinking. Put the text block as close to the lens as the app allows, because the further it sits from the lens, the more off-axis your gaze looks.

Set the scroll a touch slower than your natural pace. Chasing text is what produces the rushed, even delivery. If you are slightly ahead of the line, you will naturally pause, which is exactly what you want.

Distance helps too. At 2 metres from the phone the angle between the lens and the text is small enough to be invisible; at 40cm it is obvious.

## What do you do if you have no prompter?

Use bullet points out of frame and improvise the sentences.

Five or six keywords on a sheet taped just under the lens gives you the structure without the script. Delivery is automatically conversational, because you are constructing the sentences as you go, and the only cost is more takes.

The hybrid that works well: script the first line and the last line, bullet the middle. Openings and closings are where fumbling hurts most, and the middle benefits from sounding unrehearsed.

## How do you perform it once it is written?

Talk to one person, and let yourself stop.

Picture a specific person, not an audience. It changes your tone measurably and it is the difference between presenting and talking.

Then give yourself permission to break. If a sentence falls apart, pause two full seconds and start that sentence again from the beginning. A take with three clean restarts edits far better than one you pushed through, and the silence gives the editor an obvious cut point.

Move your hands. Gesturing pulls delivery out of reading cadence, which is why people who talk with their hands rarely sound like they are reading.

## Common questions

**Why can people tell I am reading?** Usually pacing and emphasis, not eye movement. Even tempo and stress on the wrong word are the giveaways.

**Should I memorise the script instead?** Only for short pieces. Memorised delivery has its own tell, a slightly recited quality, and it costs far more time.

**How fast should the teleprompter scroll?** Slightly slower than you speak, so you are never chasing the line.

**Where should the teleprompter text sit?** Directly beneath the lens, in a narrow column, as close to it as the app allows.

**Does distance from the camera help?** Yes. At 2 metres the gaze offset between lens and text is invisible. At arm's length it is not.
