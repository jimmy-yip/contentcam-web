How to Take a Selfie and Back Camera Photo at the Same Time

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 16 October 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026

The most flexible way to take a selfie and a back camera photo from one press is ContentCam's Front & Back photo mode, on iPhone and Android: one press saves the selfie and the rear photo as two separate files, so you choose the layout afterwards. Photo mode is Premium. For one combined image on iPhone, Snapchat's Dual Camera is free. The iPhone's Dual Capture and Instagram's Dual are video only.

Disclosure: I build ContentCam, which is one of the apps below. Claims about other apps come from their own documentation or listings, checked 5 November 2026 and listed at the end. For video, see the main front and back camera guide.

How do you get the selfie and the back camera photo as two separate files?

Use ContentCam's photo mode inside Front & Back mode, on iPhone or Android:

  1. Open ContentCam and switch the recording mode to Front & Back.
  2. Choose whether the rear camera shoots vertical or horizontal. The selfie camera is always vertical.
  3. Frame the rear shot and press the shutter button beside the record button.
  4. One press captures both cameras together and saves two photos: one from the rear camera and one from the selfie camera.

Because the photos are separate, put the selfie in any corner, set the two side by side, or post them as two carousel slides. The rear photo also stands on its own with no face covering it, which a combined image can never give you.

Photo mode is Premium in ContentCam on both iPhone and Android. The app is free to download, with no ads, no watermark and no account. Your phone must also be able to run both cameras at once; ContentCam checks this for you.

Can the iPhone Camera app take a front and back photo at the same time?

Not as a photo. Apple's iPhone User Guide puts Dual Capture in Video mode, recording "yourself" and "the action in front of you" at once, on seven models: iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max and iPhone Duo. iPhone 16 and earlier do not have it.

On those models, record a few seconds and screenshot the best frame. The selfie window is painted into that frame, so it cannot be moved or removed. More in Dual Capture's limits.

Can Snapchat take a front and back photo at the same time?

Yes, on iPhone, as one combined image. Snapchat's help centre says Dual Camera "allows you to capture content using the front and back cameras at the same time". Snapchat lists it for iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later, and its help centre still says the Android version is coming "soon".

The selfie window is part of the pixels, so you cannot move or remove it later. Snapchat's App Store listing also says "Contains Advertising", and 26 of the 250 most recent US App Store reviews (31 October to 4 November 2026) complain about ads. One 1-star review on 3 November 2026 summed it up as "More ads than content."

Can Instagram take a front and back photo?

No, only video. Meta announced Instagram's Dual on 21 July 2022 to "record using your phone's front and back cameras simultaneously". Record a short Dual clip and screenshot the best frame.

Why can't my phone take a front and back photo at the same time?

The hardware has to run both cameras together and tell apps so. On iPhone, apps check isMultiCamSupported on AVCaptureMultiCamSession (iOS 13 and later). On Android 11 (API level 30) and later, getConcurrentCameraIds() returns the cameras the phone can run at once. If a phone reports no front and rear pair, no app can capture both through the official route.

Which method should you use?

  • A carousel, a thumbnail, anything you will edit, or an Android phone: ContentCam's Front & Back photo mode: two separate photos.
  • A quick free photo for a Story on iPhone: Snapchat's Dual Camera, one fixed image.
  • An iPhone 17, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro or iPhone Duo and no extra app: a short Dual Capture video and a screenshot.

How do you make a front and back photo look good?

  • Light your face first. The selfie camera gets noisy sooner in a dim room. Face a window or a lamp.
  • Hold still for a beat. A moving hand blurs both photos at once.
  • Mind the exposure gap. With two separate files you can brighten a shaded selfie without touching a bright sky.
