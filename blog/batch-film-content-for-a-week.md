# How to Batch Film a Week of Content (2026)

Source: https://contentcam.app/blog/batch-film-content-for-a-week/

Why batch days run long and produce less than they should: splitting decide, set up, film and cut across different days, getting more than one post per setup, and logging so the footage is findable.

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 19 September 2026

Batch filming advice usually means "block out a Sunday." That is the easy half. The reason a batch day yields four posts instead of twelve is almost never the filming. Twelve short takes is about ten minutes of actual recording. The other five hours go on deciding what to say next, changing shirts, and watching takes back.

## What actually slows a batch day down?

Setup changes and decisions. Recording is the fastest part of the day.

A single short take runs 30 to 60 seconds. Twelve of them is roughly ten minutes of recording. If the day took six hours, five and a half went somewhere else: working out what the next one is about, adjusting the frame, reviewing the last take, and restarting because the opening line came out wrong.

So a batch system is not about filming faster. It is about removing everything that is not filming from the filming block.

## How should you split the work?

Into four sessions on different days, in this order.

- **Decide, 30 minutes, earlier in the week.** List what you are filming and write the first line of each. Not full scripts, just topic and opening sentence, because the opening is where takes die.
- **Set up, 15 minutes, on the day.** Frame, light, lock exposure and white balance, test audio, record ten seconds and check it. Once. Then touch nothing.
- **Film, 60 to 90 minutes.** Straight through, no reviewing.
- **Cut, a separate day.** Editing on the same day is how a two-hour block becomes an eight-hour day, and then stops happening at all.

The split matters more than the durations. Deciding and performing use different parts of your brain, and switching between them all afternoon is what makes the day feel long.

## Why should you not review takes while filming?

Because reviewing breaks rhythm, and rhythm is the whole advantage of batching.

Watching a take back puts you in an editing mindset, and getting back into performing costs several minutes and usually two or three wooden takes. Check the very first recording to confirm the setup works, then trust it. If it was right at take one it is right at take twelve, because you did not touch anything.

When a take goes wrong, do not stop the recording. Pause for two seconds and say the line again. You get a clean cut point and you keep moving.

## How do you get more than one post per setup?

Change what you say far more often than you change how you look.

A shirt change costs five minutes and buys the appearance of a different day, which is worth doing two or three times across a batch, not twelve. Cluster by outfit: film everything in the first shirt, change, film everything in the second.

The bigger multiplier is capturing more from each take than you plan to use.

- **Frame to the centre third** so the same take survives both crops, or record both formats at once. A full-width 16:9 crop of a 9:16 frame keeps only about 32% of the height, so framing decides this, not editing.
- **Grab 20 seconds of b-roll at each setup** while the light is already up. Hands, the desk, the view, you walking in. Offcuts fill gaps for months.
- **Say the same point twice**, once short and once with an example. Two usable clips, one setup.

## How do you log it so the footage is findable?

Write one line after every take, while you are still standing there. Take number, topic, usable or not. Ten seconds each.

The alternative is scrubbing two hours of unlabelled clips a week later, which costs far more than the logging did and is the single most common reason batch footage never gets edited.

If you are recording audio separately, clap once on camera at the start. And record ten seconds of silence at the end for room tone, which is what noise reduction needs to work.

## What does a realistic week look like?

- **Monday, 30 minutes.** Topics and first lines.
- **Wednesday, 2 hours.** Set up once, film everything, two outfit changes, log as you go.
- **Friday, 2 hours.** Cut, caption, schedule.

Four and a half hours for a fortnight of short-form, and only the middle block needs a tidy room and decent light.

## Common questions

**How many videos can you film in one batch session?** Ten to fifteen short pieces in about 90 minutes, once the setup is fixed and you are not reviewing between takes.

**Should you script every video?** Script the first line only. That is where most takes fail, and full scripts make delivery stiff.

**How do you stop batched videos looking batched?** Two or three outfit changes, and vary the framing between clusters. People notice the shirt long before the background.

**Should you film and edit on the same day?** No. That is what turns a manageable block into an all-day job and kills the habit.

**What is the most common batching mistake?** Reviewing each take as you go. It costs more time than anything else on this list.
