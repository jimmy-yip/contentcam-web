# Best iPhone Video Settings for TikTok (2026)

Source: https://contentcam.app/blog/best-iphone-video-settings-for-tiktok/

The iPhone settings I use for TikTok: 4K at 30 fps, HDR Video off, Auto FPS off, Lock Camera on, film in the Camera app, and turn on TikTok's Allow high-quality uploads switch before you post.

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 14 November 2026

For TikTok on an iPhone, film in the Camera app at 4K and 30 fps with HDR Video off, Auto FPS off and Lock Camera on, then upload the clip with TikTok's **Allow high-quality uploads** switch turned on. That switch sits inside TikTok, on the posting screen under More options, and most people scroll straight past it. The rest live in the iPhone Settings app under Camera.

## Which iPhone video settings should you use for TikTok?

These are the settings I use for a talking-head or walk-and-talk TikTok on an iPhone 15, 16 or 17. The iPhone menu paths come from Apple's iPhone User Guide ([Change video recording settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-video-recording-settings-iphc1827d32f/ios)). The TikTok lines come from TikTok for iPhone as it looked on 14 November 2026.

| Setting | Where it lives | Set it to | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resolution and frame rate | Settings > Camera > Record Video | 4K at 30 fps | Room to crop and punch in; 30 fps suits talking and walking shots |
| Formats | Settings > Camera > Formats | High Efficiency | HEVC files are about half the size of H.264 for the same picture |
| HDR Video | Settings > Camera > Record Video | Off | Avoids orange faces and grey skies when the clip is converted to SDR |
| Auto FPS | Settings > Camera > Record Video | Off | Stops the iPhone dropping to 24 fps indoors |
| Lock Camera | Settings > Camera > Record Video | On | Stops a visible lens switch mid take |
| Lock White Balance | Settings > Camera > Record Video | On | Skin tone stays the same colour for the whole take |
| Record Sound | Settings > Camera > Record Sound | Stereo | Plain audio that every editor and phone speaker handles the same way |
| Where you film | Camera app | Vertical, 1x lens | The Settings > Camera switches only apply in Apple's Camera app |
| Allow high-quality uploads | TikTok posting screen > More options > Advanced settings | On | The upload switch TikTok gives you |
| Data Saver | TikTok Settings and privacy > Data Saver | Off, or upload on Wi-Fi | It only acts on mobile data |

## Should you film TikToks in the iPhone Camera app or in TikTok?

Film in the Camera app and upload the clip. Every switch under Settings > Camera, from Lock Camera to HDR Video, changes Apple's Camera app only. TikTok's built-in camera makes its own choices about resolution, frame rate and lens, and shows you very few of them. If you set up your iPhone carefully and then hit record inside TikTok, none of that setup reaches the clip.

The in-app camera still has a place: duets, stitches, green screen and effects that only exist inside TikTok. For a plain talking head, a tutorial or b-roll, the Camera app gives you a better file and a copy in Photos that you keep even if the post goes wrong.

## How do you turn on high-quality uploads on TikTok?

Pick your clip, tap Next to reach the posting screen, then tap More options. Under Advanced settings there's a switch called **Allow high-quality uploads**, with the note "Higher quality videos may take longer to process". Turn it on. It sits in the same list as Allow visual search, Save to device and Save posts with watermark, so it's easy to scroll past. On my account it was already on, but check it before you post rather than assume.

Older guides call this switch "Upload HD". The name changed; it's the same idea. If you don't see it at all, update TikTok and look again, because the posting screen changes between versions.

## Does TikTok's Data Saver lower your video quality?

Data Saver is mostly about what you watch. TikTok's own description says it will "reduce your data consumption when using mobile data", that videos "may be at a lower resolution or take longer to load", and that it "won't apply when you're on Wi-Fi". So it's worth switching off if your feed looks soft on mobile data. For uploads, the simpler habit is to post on Wi-Fi, where the switch does nothing either way. It lives in TikTok under Settings and privacy, then Data Saver.

## Should you film TikTok in 4K or 1080p on iPhone?

4K at 30 fps if you have the storage, 1080p at 30 fps if you don't. TikTok re-encodes every upload, so viewers never see your 4K file as 4K. The value is in the edit: a 4K vertical frame is 2160 pixels wide, so you can punch in 2x on a face for a second angle and still have a full 1080 pixel picture. In my own clips, 4K at 30 fps in HEVC ran 169 to 275 MB a minute against about 60 MB for 1080p. ## What frame rate is best for TikTok on iPhone?

30 fps for anything played at normal speed, 60 fps only for footage you'll slow down. A 60 fps clip dropped onto a 30 fps timeline plays at half speed, smoothly. Played at full speed, 60 fps gives a talking head that glossy home-video look and needs twice the light per frame. 24 fps judders on walking shots and fast pans, which TikTok is full of. If you film under 50 Hz lights and see flicker at 30, try 25 fps. [24fps vs 30fps vs 60fps for social media](https://contentcam.app/blog/24fps-vs-30fps-vs-60fps-social-media/) has the full table.

## Why does my TikTok look blurry after uploading?

Usually one of five things, roughly in the order I'd check them:

- **Allow high-quality uploads is off.** Check it on the posting screen under More options.
- **HDR Video was on.** The clip gets converted from HDR to SDR somewhere between your camera roll and the feed, and faces come out orange or washed out. [Why iPhone video looks orange or washed out](https://contentcam.app/blog/iphone-video-orange-washed-out-hdr/) covers fixing clips you've already filmed.
- **The room was dim.** Noise uses up the bits a compressor has to spend, so a dark clip falls apart on upload. A lamp beside the phone helps more than any setting.
- **The file isn't your original.** A screen recording, a clip saved from another app, or a TikTok downloaded and re-uploaded has already been compressed once. Upload the original from Photos.
- **Auto FPS dropped to 24 fps.** It reads as choppy, not blurry, but people describe both the same way. Turn Auto FPS off.

## What about iPhone 16 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro?

The same settings apply. Two things Pro owners ask about: ProRes and Apple Log are for colour-graded edits on a computer, and their files are many times larger than HEVC, which TikTok re-encodes anyway, so leave them off for TikTok. And from iPhone 16 the camera records Spatial Audio by default, so set Record Sound to Stereo for a plain file that every editor handles the same way. ## Common questions

**Does TikTok's camera use my iPhone camera settings?** No. The switches in Settings > Camera belong to Apple's Camera app. TikTok's built-in camera makes its own choices, so film in the Camera app and upload the clip if you want the settings in this post to apply.

**Where is the high-quality upload setting on TikTok?** On the posting screen, tap More options, then look under Advanced settings for Allow high-quality uploads. TikTok notes that higher quality videos may take longer to process.

**Does Data Saver make my TikTok uploads blurry?** TikTok describes Data Saver as reducing data use on mobile data, with videos that may be lower resolution or slower to load, and says it does not apply on Wi-Fi. Upload on Wi-Fi and it is out of the picture.

**Should I upload TikToks in 4K?** Film in 4K if you have the storage, because it gives you room to crop and punch in. TikTok re-encodes every upload and does not publish its playback resolution in its help centre, so I am not quoting a number for what viewers receive.

**Do these settings work for Reels and YouTube Shorts?** Yes. The iPhone side is the same for every vertical platform. Only the upload switch is TikTok specific.
