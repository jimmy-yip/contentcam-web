DoubleTake vs ContentCam: An Honest Comparison (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 7 September 2026

Disclosure first: I build ContentCam, one of the two apps here, so I'm not neutral. Every factual claim about DoubleTake below comes from its own App Store listing, checked 7 September 2026, and I've said where a number came from. If something's out of date, email me and I'll correct it.

DoubleTake launched in January 2020 and was the first app most iPhone owners ever saw record two cameras at the same time. It mattered. A lot of the multicam phone filming people do now traces back to it.

Two things have changed since. Filmic was acquired, and DoubleTake is now published by Bending Spoons Apps ApS. And the app has not shipped an update in a while: the listing shows version 3.1.2, last updated March 2025.

It still works. It is still free to download. But if you're choosing a filming app in 2026, there are specifics worth knowing before you commit a shoot to it.

These two apps solve different problems

Worth getting clear before any feature table, because the two apps get shelved together constantly.

DoubleTake records two cameras at once. Your wide lens and your telephoto, or the front and the back. You get two angles of the same moment.

ContentCam records two aspect ratios at once. One scene, captured as a 9:16 vertical file and a 16:9 horizontal file in a single take, so the same footage is ready for TikTok and for YouTube without filming it twice.

If you want a reaction shot alongside your POV, that's a multicam job. If you're posting the same video to a vertical platform and a horizontal one, that's a dual-format job. They overlap, but they are not the same need, and picking the wrong category is how people end up disappointed.

ContentCam does also have a Front / Back mode, which records both cameras together. So there's genuine overlap. The difference is which problem each app was built around first.

Where DoubleTake genuinely wins

It's free to start, and the core feature isn't paywalled to death. You can download it and record two cameras without paying anything. That's a real advantage over a lot of the category.

It reaches back further on old hardware. Its listing names devices as old as the iPhone Xs, Xs Max and Xr for multicam. If you're filming on a phone from 2018, that matters more than any feature list.

iPad and Apple Vision support. ContentCam doesn't have that.

It does one thing and doesn't clutter the screen. There's something to be said for an app that opens, records two angles, and gets out of the way.

The specifics worth knowing before a shoot

1080p is the ceiling. The listing states 1080p at high bitrate encoding. In 2026, most phones shoot 4K by default, and most platforms accept it. If you crop, reframe or stabilise in the edit, starting at 1080p costs you room you can't get back.

Frame rates stop at 30. The listing names 24, 25 and 30fps. No 60fps, so no smooth slow motion and no 60fps delivery.

The device list ends at the iPhone 13 series. Newer phones aren't named. That doesn't mean it won't run on them, but it does mean nobody has updated the listing to say it does.

March 2025 was the last update. That's the single most useful signal here. It doesn't make the app bad, and plenty of stable software goes quiet. It does mean you're betting on something that hasn't been touched in a while, on a platform where each iOS release moves the ground.

The rating sits at 3.4 stars from 503 ratings, which is worth reading alongside the update history rather than on its own.

How it's priced

DoubleTake is free with in-app purchases. The listing shows subscriptions ranging from weekly options at $1.49 to $9.99 and yearly options at $19.99 to $99.99, across several bundle configurations. Which tier you land on depends on what you're offered in-app, so check before subscribing.

ContentCam has monthly and annual subscriptions with a 7-day free trial, and a one-time lifetime option for people who would rather not add another subscription. Current pricing is here.

So which should you download?

Get DoubleTake if your phone is older than an iPhone 13, you want two camera angles rather than two aspect ratios, you're filming on an iPad, or 1080p at 30fps genuinely covers what you deliver.

Get ContentCam if you post the same video to both a vertical and a horizontal platform and you're tired of reshooting or cropping, you want 4K, you need a teleprompter, or you're on Android.

Neither answer is a knock on the other app. DoubleTake got there first and earned its reputation. It's just aimed at a different job than the one most cross-posting creators have in 2026.
