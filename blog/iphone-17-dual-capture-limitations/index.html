iPhone 17 Dual Capture (2026): What Does It Do, and Where Does It Stop?

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 28 September 2026

Disclosure first: I build ContentCam, a camera app that also records two things at once, so Dual Capture is the feature people compare mine to. That makes me an interested party, which is why every number below is tied to an Apple page. The iPhone 17, iPhone Air and iPhone 18 Pro specs pages and the iOS 27 iPhone User Guide were all read on 28 September 2026.

Dual Capture is good, it is free, and it is already on the phone. It also has hard edges Apple leaves off the marketing page.

What is iPhone 17 Dual Capture, and what does it record?

Dual Capture records the front and rear cameras at the same time, in the stock Camera app, and Apple's published ceiling for it is one line: "Dual Capture up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps". That exact string appears in the Video Recording section of the rear-camera specs for iPhone 17, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo. The Front Camera section of the same pages lists "Dual Capture" with no figure beside it.

Apple's iOS 27 iPhone User Guide page Record a video with your iPhone camera says the mode lets you "record yourself" and "the action in front of you" at the same time. You reach it by opening Camera, selecting Video mode, tapping the camera-controls button, then tapping Dual Capture. Apple's announcement on 9 September 2025 put it plainly: "users can now record on the front and rear cameras simultaneously with Dual Capture".

The layout is the part worth reading carefully. The illustration Apple uses in that guide page is captioned, in its own alt text: "A window in the top-left of the frame displays what the front-facing camera is recording, and the rest of the frame displays what the back camera is recording." One frame. Front camera inset, rear camera behind it.

Which iPhones support Dual Capture?

Six models, and Apple publishes the list. The iOS 27 iPhone User Guide has a page titled Models that support Dual Capture video, and on 28 September 2026 it reads:

  • iPhone 17
  • iPhone 17 Pro
  • iPhone 17 Pro Max
  • iPhone Air
  • iPhone 18 Pro
  • iPhone 18 Pro Max

The iPhone Duo specs page also carries the "Dual Capture up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps" line, so treat that as seven in practice. Apple's guide list has not caught up with it yet.

Two models people assume have Dual Capture do not. The iPhone 16 specs page and the iPhone 17e specs page contain no mention of Dual Capture anywhere. If your iPhone is not on Apple's list, the stock Camera app will not do this and no iOS update changes that, because it is a hardware line rather than a software one. The dual camera effects built into TikTok, Snapchat and Instagram run on far older phones, and third-party camera apps have recorded front and rear together since iOS 13.

Why is Dual Capture capped at 30 fps when the same iPhone shoots 4K60?

Apple does not publish a reason. What the specs page does show is the size of the drop, on one page. iPhone 17 rear camera, from the specs page read 28 September 2026:

ModeApple's published ceiling
Normal video recording4K Dolby Vision at 24, 25, 30 or 60 fps
QuickTake videoup to 4K Dolby Vision at 60 fps
Action modeup to 2.8K Dolby Vision at 60 fps
Slo-mo1080p at 120 fps or 240 fps
Dual Captureup to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps

The same phone that hands you 4K60 from one camera gives you 4K30 the moment the second camera joins in. For a talking-head post that does not matter, because 30 fps is the right frame rate for almost all short-form anyway. It matters if you wanted to slow anything down, because a 30 fps capture has no slow motion in it.

Running two cameras at once is rationed at the operating-system level, and not only inside Apple's Camera app. Third-party apps have had AVCaptureMultiCamSession since iOS 13.0, and Apple's documentation for isMultiCamSupported, the flag that decides which capture formats a multi-camera session may use, says: "Only this limited subset of capture formats can run sustainably in a multi-camera capture scenario." The limit is tighter than Dual Capture's. Every iPhone ContentCam has run on, Pro models included, tops out at 1920×1440 for formats that report isMultiCamSupported. Android draws the same line and writes it down: the CameraDevice reference guarantees concurrent streams only at s1440p, which it defines as "the camera device's maximum resolution for that format from StreamConfigurationMap.getOutputSizes or 1440p(1920X1440) whichever is lower". So Dual Capture at 4K30 is ahead of what any other app on the phone can offer, mine included.

Does Dual Capture give you two separate video files?

No. Everything Apple documents points to one composited recording with the picture-in-picture baked into the frame: a single Video Recording spec line, a single frame in the illustration, and a caption describing the front camera as a window inside that frame rather than as a second recording.

Apple publishes no statement about the file count either way, and I could not find one on apple.com, in the iOS 27 iPhone User Guide or in the developer documentation. So treat the layout as permanent until Apple says otherwise, and plan the shot on that basis. Confirming it takes ten seconds: record five seconds of Dual Capture, open Photos, count the items.

What can you not do with a Dual Capture video afterwards?

Four things, and they all follow from the single composited frame.

  1. You cannot cut to one angle. The inset is painted over the top-left corner of every frame, so there is no clean rear-camera-only shot anywhere in the file.
  2. You cannot move or resize the inset later. Its position is decided at capture and is part of the pixels from then on. The TikTok, Snapchat and Instagram dual effects behave the same way, so this is normal for the category rather than an Apple failing.
  3. You cannot reframe for a second platform without losing the inset. This is the one that catches people. Crop a 3840×2160 landscape take to 9:16 for TikTok and you keep 1215×2160, which is about 32% of the pixels and none of the left edge where the face was. Crop a vertical take to 16:9 and you lose the top and bottom instead. One capture, one aspect ratio, and a wide version that is a crop rather than a wide shot.
  4. You cannot grade the two feeds separately. One file, one colour pipeline. If the rear camera is outdoors at 5600K and your face is lit by a warm bulb indoors, the mismatch is fixed at capture.

None of this is a bug. It is what a finished-post feature is supposed to do, and Apple has built exactly that.

When is Dual Capture the right choice?

When the clip you record is the clip you post. In that case Dual Capture beats every alternative on the phone, mine included, and I will say so plainly:

  • Nothing to install, nothing to pay, nothing to learn. Three taps inside an app already on the Home Screen, saving straight to Photos.
  • 4K30 Dolby Vision, from Apple's own capture pipeline. No third-party app on iOS can record 4K with two cameras running, because no iPhone reports a 4K capture format as multi-cam supported. Dual Capture can.
  • The Center Stage front camera doing the selfie half. Apple's 9 September 2025 announcement calls it "the first square front camera sensor on iPhone, offering a wider field of view and higher resolution", and the iPhone 17 specs page lists an "18MP Center Stage camera". A wider front field of view is what a small inset frame needs.
  • Reaction clips, quick pieces to camera and anything going up as a single vertical post. There is no reason to open another app for those.

What should you use when Dual Capture is not enough?

A third-party camera app, and the trade is explicit: give up 4K on two cameras to get the two feeds as separate footage. Every front and back app on iPhone sits under the same 1920×1440 multi-camera ceiling, so HD is the honest expectation from all of them.

ContentCam is my answer to that, and here is the accurate version. Front & Back mode records the front and rear cameras at the same time and saves two separate video files, one per camera, never a composite and never a picture-in-picture. That holds on iPhone and on Android. Because it runs through Apple's multi-camera session on iOS and Android's concurrent-camera contract on Android, Front & Back and Dual Lens are HD only, and 4K and 60 fps are single-lens only. Dual Lens, which records the wide and ultra-wide rear cameras together, needs multi-camera-capable hardware, so it is iPhone 11 and later, and it is capped at 1080p for the same 1920×1440 reason. No Android phone has advertised a concurrent rear-plus-rear pair to date, so Dual Lens is iPhone only.

Two separate files is the whole point of it: cut to either angle, grade them independently, keep one and bin the other, or line them up on a timeline. A composited frame allows none of that. If you do not need any of it, use Dual Capture. It is free and it is already on the phone.

Frequently asked questions

What frame rate does iPhone 17 Dual Capture record at? Apple's specs pages list "Dual Capture up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps". The same rear camera records normal video at up to 4K Dolby Vision at 60 fps, so Dual Capture costs you half the frame rate.

Does Dual Capture work on iPhone 16? No. The iPhone 16 specs page does not mention Dual Capture, and Apple's list of models that support it starts at iPhone 17. The iPhone 17e is not on the list either.

Does Dual Capture save two files? Everything Apple documents describes one composited frame with the front camera as a window in the top-left corner. Apple publishes no statement on the file count, so plan for one file and check your own Photos library to confirm.

Can you move the picture-in-picture window in Dual Capture? Apple's published steps for the mode do not mention repositioning or resizing it, and I could not verify any layout control against an Apple source. Other coverage says the window can be moved during recording. Either way, wherever it sits when you stop recording is where it stays.

Can third-party iPhone apps record 4K with two cameras at once? No. Apple restricts multi-camera capture to formats where isMultiCamSupported is true, and no iPhone ContentCam has run on reports one above 1920×1440. Dual Capture in the stock Camera app is the only 4K two-camera option on iOS.

Does Android have the same resolution ceiling? Yes, at the same number. Android exposes concurrent front and rear capture through the Camera2 getConcurrentCameraIds() contract, and Google's CameraDevice reference guarantees those concurrent streams only at s1440p, defined as 1920×1440 or the camera's own maximum, whichever is lower.
