## How much time does ContentCam save?

Most of ContentCam's value is time. Without it, a video that has to go to TikTok, Reels or Shorts and to YouTube means one of two things: film everything twice, once vertical and once horizontal, or film once and crop, which throws away about two thirds of the frame and leaves the second version tight and soft.

Filming twice costs more than the second take itself. You reset the phone, the framing and the lighting, then try to match your delivery, your energy and every line of the first take, and the second one rarely comes out as good. Then you edit and trim two separate timelines. As a simple example, if you film 30 minutes a week that has to go out in both formats, a second pass costs at least another 30 minutes a week, which is more than 26 hours a year before any editing.

With ContentCam you film once. Both files come from the same take, so your delivery is identical in both. You review them side by side and trim them together before saving. Every take is filed under its project and scene and named for you (for example my_project_scene_001_Portrait.mov), so there's no hunting through the camera roll for the right version. For most creators who post both formats, a few weeks of saved filming time is worth more than a year of Premium.

## When is ContentCam not worth paying for?

- **You only post vertical video.** The point of ContentCam is getting both formats from one take. If you never need a horizontal version, the free tier may be all you want, or your phone's camera may be enough.
- **You need 4K from two cameras at once.** Front & Back records both cameras in HD, because phone multi-camera formats top out there. 4K is available in the single-camera modes.
- **You're on an older iPhone.** ContentCam needs iOS 18, which means iPhone XS, XR or newer.
- **You want 60fps Front & Back on Android.** On Android, Front & Back records HD at up to 30fps, on phones that can run both cameras together.
