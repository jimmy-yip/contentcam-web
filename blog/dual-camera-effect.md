## What is the dual camera effect?

It's a video where the front and back cameras record at once: the main shot fills the screen and your face sits in a small inset, or the two are stacked as a split screen. It's the look behind most reaction videos and walking vlogs, and because both share one timeline, the reaction timing is real.

## Why record the two cameras as separate files?

Every built-in option bakes the layout into one file at capture. Separate files let you:

- **Place the inset later.** Move it, resize it, or hide it when it covers the shot.
- **Cut between angles.** Full screen on your face for the reaction, then full screen on the scene.
- **Keep clean B-roll** with no face on it.
- **Edit anywhere.** Instagram and Snapchat give you one combined clip, not two files for CapCut or Premiere.

## How do you use Dual Capture on iPhone?

Per Apple's iPhone User Guide: open Camera, select Video, tap the Camera Controls button, tap **Dual Capture**, then tap the screen and record. Apple lists seven models (in the table above); iPhone 16 and earlier, iPhone 16e and iPhone 17e don't have it. More in [iPhone 17 Dual Capture and where it stops](https://contentcam.app/blog/iphone-17-dual-capture-limitations/).

## How do you use both cameras at the same time on Android?

Use a camera app with a front and back mode, or check Instagram's Dual. Apple's Dual Capture is iPhone only, and Snapchat's help centre still says Dual Camera "will be available on Android soon". Hardware decides the rest: Android 11 added getConcurrentCameraIds(), which reports which cameras can run together, and ContentCam only offers Front & Back where it works.

## Why is the Instagram dual camera not showing?

Meta announced Dual in July 2022 as a Reels tool to "record using your phone's front and back cameras simultaneously". If it's missing, update Instagram, look in the Reels camera and then Stories, and check the phone can run two cameras at once. Failing that, record with [Dual Capture] or a front and back camera app (see [the dual-camera apps compared](https://contentcam.app/blog/best-front-and-back-dual-camera-apps/)) and upload the clip as a Reel.

## Can you get the dual camera effect in portrait and landscape?

Not with the built-in modes, which record one aspect ratio; cropping vertical to 16:9 keeps about a third of the frame. ContentCam's core mode records a scene [as a vertical file and a wide file at the same time](https://contentcam.app/blog/record-portrait-and-landscape-video-same-time-iphone/), so you can shoot for [TikTok and YouTube without reshooting](https://contentcam.app/blog/film-for-tiktok-and-youtube-at-the-same-time/).

## Common questions

**How do you film the front and back camera at the same time?** Turn on a dual camera mode before you record. On iPhone 17, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro or iPhone Duo that is Dual Capture in the Camera app. On other iPhones running iOS 18 or later and on Android phones that can run both cameras, a front and back camera app such as ContentCam records both as separate files.

**Do I need an iPhone 17 to use both cameras at once?** No. Dual Capture needs an iPhone 17 model, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro model or iPhone Duo. On iPhone 15, iPhone 16 and other iPhones on iOS 18 or later, ContentCam's Front & Back mode records both cameras, and Snapchat's Dual Camera works from iPhone XS and XR.

**Is the dual camera effect free?** The built-in versions are: Instagram's Dual, Snapchat's Dual Camera and the iPhone's Dual Capture each give you one finished file with the layout fixed. ContentCam's Front & Back gives 5 free recordings as two separate files, then needs Premium.

**Can I move the small frame afterwards?** Not with the built-in options, which bake the layout into one file. You need the two cameras saved as separate files, which is what ContentCam's Front & Back mode records.
