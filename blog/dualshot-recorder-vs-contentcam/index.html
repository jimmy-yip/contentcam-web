DualShot Recorder vs ContentCam: Which Is Better in 2026?

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 29 July 2026 · Updated 13 November 2026

I'm Jimmy and I build ContentCam. I tested DualShot Recorder 3.1.2 myself on 13 November 2026; everything marked "tested" comes from that session, and the rest from its App Store listing, its developer's website and its App Store reviews. Where I give an opinion, I flag it as mine. If anything's out of date, email me and I'll fix it.

For most people ContentCam is the better pick: it records 9:16 and 16:9 from one take on iPhone and Android, adds no watermark on the free tier, records the selfie and rear cameras at the same time, and still sells a one-time lifetime purchase. DualShot Recorder (also written "Dual Shot Recorder"), made by DDJR Productions, records the same two formats on iPhone only.

Does DualShot Recorder put a watermark on free videos?

Yes. DDJR Productions' own website says DualShot Recorder's free Basic tier records at 1080p and 30fps, caps each clip at 60 seconds and adds a DualShot watermark; the Advanced subscription removes it. That watermark is the most common complaint in recent reviews: 4 of the last 50 US App Store reviews (22 June to 28 October 2026) are about watermarked footage, including buyers who paid for version 2. One 1-star review (1 October 2026) says "you've spent hours filming footage with a watermark that can't be removed". ContentCam adds no watermark and shows no ads, on the free tier or Premium.

Has DualShot Recorder lost recordings?

Some users report it: in the same 50 reviews, 3 describe recordings that stopped or never saved, and 2 describe audio out of sync. A 1-star review from 18 July 2026 reads "constantly crashing and not saving/restoring recorded content". DualShot Recorder's last update, 3.1.2 on 5 June 2026, is listed only as "Bug fixes and performance improvements", and the last version that added features was 3.0 on 2 May 2026 (teleprompter, manual ISO, stabilisation). Whichever app you choose, record a test take before a paid shoot.

When I tested DualShot Recorder 3.1.2 myself on 13 November 2026, a take was lost every time the phone locked, the app closed or a call came in, and 4K at 60 fps saved at 38 to 39 fps. On 14 November a time-lapse also failed to save, with "PHPhotosErrorDomain error 3302" (Photos rejected the file as invalid), and the footage was lost. The full results, with screenshots, are in my DualShot Recorder review.

Can ContentCam record two cameras at once?

Yes, in two ways. Dual Lens records the wide and ultra-wide cameras at the same time on iPhone 11 and later, so the portrait and landscape files can be genuinely different framings. Front & Back mode records the selfie and rear cameras at the same time as two separate files, on every iPhone that runs iOS 18 (including iPhone 15 and iPhone 16) and on Android phones that support it; the free tier includes 5 Front & Back recordings. In single-lens mode, ContentCam records the sensor's full 4:3 frame and cuts both formats from it, so the landscape keeps the full width instead of being a centre strip. DualShot Recorder records one camera at a time: the "front and back camera support" in its listing means either camera can be used, and its site puts it as "Front or back camera, your call".

Which app is more reliable?

In my test of DualShot Recorder 3.1.2 on 13 November 2026, a take was lost every time I locked the phone, went to the home screen, force-closed the app, opened Siri or took a call. 4K set to 60fps saved at 38.19 to 39.23fps, with choppy motion. Free takes saved at 24fps although its website promises 30, and the frame rate and lens settings reset between takes. With cinematic stabilisation on, the picture-in-picture ran about 2 seconds behind. The record button also sits left of centre, which a 17 August 2026 App Store review mentions too.

ContentCam keeps recording through auto-lock and restores a force-closed take the next time you open it, and on iPhone every take can also be reached in the Files app. Its preview stays in real time with cinematic stabilisation on, and the free version has no watermark. In my opinion it's the more complete tool too: Dual Lens, Front & Back, the full manual panel (exposure, white balance, ISO and shutter), and a project, scene and take workflow with automatic file naming. Screenshots of every DualShot Recorder result are in my DualShot Recorder review.

Can you still buy DualShot Recorder once?

Not as a new buyer. DualShot Recorder launched as a one-time purchase; version 3.1 (26 May 2026) made it free to download with optional subscriptions, and its App Store listing now shows only "Advanced Annual" and "Advance Monthly" in-app purchases. Version 2 buyers keep Advanced, per DDJR Productions, and its website says the app detects the old purchase automatically. In practice, at least 17 App Store reviews since May say the app didn't recognise their purchase, and comments under DualShot Recorder's Instagram posts and in its Telegram support group describe emails that went unanswered, some since September. DualShot Recorder hasn't been updated since 5 June 2026. ContentCam keeps a one-time lifetime purchase alongside yearly and monthly plans, and all three get the same features.
