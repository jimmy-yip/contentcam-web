DualShot Recorder (you'll also see it written "Dual Shot Recorder") is the app that put "record vertical and horizontal at the same time" on the map in early 2026. Credit where it's due: it got big fast. A lot of that came from its creator already having a sizeable online following. That's real, hard-won distribution, and it's why you've probably heard of it. But a big launch audience and the better app aren't the same thing. So let's actually compare them.

The big difference: single lens vs single and dual lens

This is the one that surprises people. DualShot Recorder is single-lens only. It records one camera and produces the two crops from that. ContentCam does that too (it's the free mode), but on iPhone 11 and later it also has Dual Lens mode: the wide and ultra-wide cameras recording at the same time, so your portrait and landscape can be genuinely different framings: a tight vertical and a wide establishing shot from one tap.

And in single-lens mode, ContentCam records the sensor's full 4:3 frame and cuts both formats from it, so the landscape keeps its full width at full resolution instead of being a centre strip. That's the difference between a real wide shot and a zoomed-in one.

Polish and completeness (my honest opinion)

Feature lists only get you so far; how the app feels matters when you use it every day. Having spent real time in both, this is where I think the gap is widest. And yes, I'm biased, so take it as one developer's read and go try them yourself:

  • ContentCam is the more finished app. Dual Lens, the full manual panel (exposure, white balance, ISO and shutter), the project/scene/take workflow with automatic file naming, side-by-side review. It's built to be the complete tool, not a one-trick demo. I've put a lot of care into it, and I genuinely think it's the best dual-camera recording app out there right now.
  • DualShot feels more basic in use. In my testing the picture-in-picture preview lags, the record button sits slightly off-centre, and the interface is sparser overall. None of that is fatal, but the little rough edges add up when you shoot regularly. Try it and judge for yourself.

Where DualShot Recorder genuinely wins

No hedging. These are real advantages today:

  • Apple Log. If you grade seriously, DualShot captures Log and ContentCam doesn't yet. For a colourist that can be the whole decision.
  • Cheaper subscription. Its annual plan undercuts ContentCam's. If you're iPhone-only and price is the deciding factor, that's fair. Check both store listings for the current figures in your region.
  • Name recognition. It's the one people have heard of. That's worth something if you just want the app everyone's talking about.

The pricing shift worth knowing about

One thing that isn't on the surface: DualShot Recorder launched as a one-time purchase, and has since moved to subscription only, with no lifetime option anymore. That's their call to make, and subscriptions are normal. But if you're the kind of person who'd rather buy a tool once than rent it, it's worth knowing the one-time option is gone there. ContentCam deliberately keeps a lifetime option (one payment, forever) alongside the subscriptions. I ship new features to it for free, and I'd rather you own the app than rent it.
