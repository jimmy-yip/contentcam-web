How to Switch Camera While Recording on iPhone (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 3 November 2026

You can't switch between the front and back camera mid-recording in the iPhone's own Camera app. Apple's iPhone User Guide lists what you can do while a video is recording, and flipping the camera is not on the list. There are three ways around it: stop and start a new clip on the other camera, use Dual Capture on an iPhone 17 or later to record both cameras at once, or record both cameras into separate files with a third-party app and cut between them in the edit. The last one is the only way to get a clean "cut to my face, cut back to the view" result from a single take.

Can you switch camera while recording on iPhone?

Not in Apple's Camera app. Apple's page Record a video with your iPhone camera (iOS 27 edition, checked 4 November 2026) says that while recording you can tap the white Shutter button to take a still photo, tap Pause, pinch to zoom, or tap the .5x, 1x, 2x and other zoom buttons. Those zoom buttons change rear lenses, the ultra wide, main and telephoto. None of them reach the front camera, and the guide describes no control that swaps the front camera and the back camera during a take.

That holds for every model from iPhone 11 to iPhone 18 Pro, because it's a Camera app limit rather than a hardware one. The search "switch camera while recording iPhone 17 Pro" has the same answer as "iPhone 13".

Not to be confused with the iPhone switching rear lenses on its own while you film, usually when you get close to a subject. That's a different problem with its own fix: why the iPhone camera switches lenses while recording.

Why can't you switch camera while recording on iPhone?

Because a recording is one continuous file from one camera. Swapping cameras means the phone has to stop one sensor, start another with a different resolution, field of view and colour, and keep writing to the same file without a gap. Apple's Camera app doesn't attempt that. Where an app does let you flip mid-take, expect a brief jump at the switch, because one camera has to stop before the other one starts.

How do you switch cameras by stopping and starting?

This is the free method that works on every iPhone:

  1. Record the first part on the back camera and tap Record to stop.
  2. Tap the flip button (the circular arrows, bottom right) to switch to the front camera.
  3. Record the next part, then stop.
  4. Join the clips in an editor. iMovie is free from Apple and lays clips end to end on one timeline; CapCut does the same.

Two tips make the joins invisible. Finish each clip on a natural pause, a breath or a look away, so the cut lands on silence. And keep the phone in the same orientation for every clip, otherwise one part comes out sideways.

The limit: nothing in clip one happens at the same moment as clip two. For a reaction, an unboxing or a "watch this" moment, that gap is the whole problem, because a face filmed after the event reads as acted.

How does Dual Capture work instead of switching?

Dual Capture records the front camera and the back camera together in Apple's Camera app, so there is nothing to switch. Open Camera, choose Video, tap the camera-controls button, then tap Dual Capture, per the same Apple guide. Apple's specs pages list it at up to 4K Dolby Vision at 30 fps.

The catch is the layout. Apple's guide illustration shows the front camera in a window at the top left of the frame with the rear camera filling the rest, and everything Apple publishes points to one file with that window painted in. You can't cut to a clean rear-only shot later or move the window. The models, the 30 fps cap and the other limits are in which iPhones have Dual Capture, and where it stops.

How do you switch between front and back camera in the edit instead?

Record both cameras into separate files, then cut between them like a two-camera shoot. This is how TV does it, and it gives the result people searching "switch camera while recording" usually want: the view, then your face at the exact moment you reacted, then the view again.

  1. Record front and back at the same time in an app that saves each camera as its own file. Third-party apps have been able to run two iPhone cameras at once since Apple added AVCaptureMultiCamSession in iOS 13.
  2. Clap once at the start with both cameras rolling. The spike in the audio waveform gives you a sync point if the editor needs one.
  3. Stack the two clips in iMovie, CapCut or any editor with layers, rear camera on the main track and front camera above it, lined up on the clap.
  4. Cut the top layer wherever you want the front camera to show, and delete the rest of it. Every cut is a "camera switch", and you can move them after the fact.

The trade-off is resolution. Apple caps the formats a multi-camera session can use, and on every iPhone ContentCam has run on, Pro models included, those formats top out at 1920×1440. So two-file front and back recording on iPhone is an HD job, not 4K.

ContentCam, which I build, does it this way: Front & Back mode records the selfie camera and the rear camera together and saves them as two separate files, on iPhone and Android. Since version 2.8 the iPhone app can also combine a Front & Back take into one video with picture-in-picture or a split screen, with the original two files left untouched, so you can post the combined version and still cut between the angles in another edit.

Which apps let you flip the camera mid-recording?

The cameras built into social apps such as Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok have their own flip controls, and some of them let you flip during a take. The behaviour changes between app versions, so test it before you rely on it, and expect a small jump at the flip. On Snapchat, Snapchat Support says the flip button still works inside Dual Camera, where it swaps which camera fills the main view.

The footage stays inside that app until you post or save it, usually as one file with the switch already made. For anything going to more than one platform, recording both cameras and choosing the cuts later gives you more to work with.

Common questions

Can you switch from the back camera to the front camera while recording on iPhone 17? Not in the Camera app. Use Dual Capture to record both at once, or stop, flip and record a second clip.

Does pausing a recording let you flip the camera? Apple's guide lists Pause as something you can do while recording, but describes no camera swap while paused. If you need two cameras in one video, use Dual Capture or two files.

Can you switch cameras while recording on Android? It depends on the phone maker's camera app, so check whether the flip button stays on screen after you press record. Some Samsung Galaxy phones have Director's View with Dual recording, which records front and back together. Recording front and back at the same time on Android has the details.

Why does my video jump when I flip the camera? Because the app stopped one camera and started the other mid-take. Recording both cameras at once avoids it, since neither camera ever stops.
