Is ContentCam Worth It? What's Free and What Premium Adds (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 10 November 2026 · Updated 12 November 2026

ContentCam is worth it if you post the same video both vertically and horizontally, or film your reaction and the scene in front of you at the same time. You can find out for free: the free version records 9:16 and 16:9 at once in 1080p, with no limit on recordings, no ads, no watermark and no account. Premium is worth paying for when you need 4K, 60fps, unlimited Front & Back and Dual Lens, manual exposure controls, the teleprompter, photo mode or timelapse. If you only ever post vertical video and your phone's own camera does the job, you don't need it.

I build ContentCam, so weigh that. Everything below comes from the app's current free and Premium tiers and its public App Store reviews, checked 10 November 2026.

How much time does ContentCam save?

Most of ContentCam's value is time. Without it, a video that has to go to TikTok, Reels or Shorts and to YouTube means one of two things: film everything twice, once vertical and once horizontal, or film once and crop, which throws away about two thirds of the frame and leaves the second version tight and soft.

Filming twice costs more than the second take itself. You reset the phone, the framing and the lighting, then try to match your delivery, your energy and every line of the first take, and the second one rarely comes out as good. Then you edit and trim two separate timelines. As a simple example, if you film 30 minutes a week that has to go out in both formats, a second pass costs at least another 30 minutes a week, which is more than 26 hours a year before any editing.

With ContentCam you film once. Both files come from the same take, so your delivery is identical in both. You review them side by side and trim them together before saving. Every take is filed under its project and scene and named for you (for example my_project_scene_001_Portrait.mov), so there's no hunting through the camera roll for the right version. For most creators who post both formats, a few weeks of saved filming time is worth more than a year of Premium.

What do you get in ContentCam for free?

  • Unlimited portrait and landscape recording. Single Lens records the full 4:3 sensor and saves a 9:16 file and a 16:9 file from the same take, so neither is a crop of the other.
  • 1080p at 24, 25 or 30fps, with stabilisation (plus HDR on iPhone), focus and exposure lock, and pause and resume.
  • The front camera in both formats, for talking-head videos.
  • 5 Dual Lens recordings (wide and ultra-wide at once, iPhone 11 or later) and 5 Front & Back recordings, so you can try both on your own footage.
  • 3 projects, with every take named for you, and a side-by-side review of both files.
  • No ads, no watermark, no account.

What does ContentCam Premium add?

  • 4K, and 50 or 60fps.
  • Unlimited Dual Lens and Front & Back. Front & Back saves your face and the scene as two separate files, and Combine turns them into a picture-in-picture or split-screen video afterwards while keeping the originals.
  • Manual controls: white balance, ISO and shutter, plus background blur on Android (beta).
  • The teleprompter, which scrolls your script while you record both formats.
  • Photo mode and timelapse.
  • Trim before saving, unlimited projects, and every new feature that comes out. The app updates every one to two weeks; the full list is on the changelog.

The Premium camera features also work in the live preview. The upgrade screen only appears when you press record with one switched on, so you can see exactly what you'd be paying for before you pay.

What else comes with ContentCam?

Beyond the two formats, it's a full creator camera:

  • Teleprompter: paste a script of up to 15,000 characters and it scrolls over the camera view at your speed, size and colour, with mirror mode for teleprompter rigs, so your eyes stay near the lens.
  • Manual controls: exposure, white balance, ISO and shutter, tap to focus, hold for AE/AF lock, a grid, a level and an audio meter.
  • Front & Back: your reaction and the scene as two separate files, then Combine turns them into one video: picture-in-picture with a window you drag and resize (rounded or square corners), or split screen with one camera stacked above the other, and you choose which camera is the big one.
  • Dual Lens: wide and ultra-wide at once for two different shots of the same moment (iPhone 11 or later).
  • Photo mode (a 9:16 and a 16:9 photo in one tap), timelapse from 10x to 900x, and 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60fps.
  • A library that stays organised: projects, scenes and takes, your choice of folder, pause and resume in the same take, and interrupted takes recovered the next time you open the app.

What makes ContentCam different from other dual-format camera apps?

  • iPhone and Android. The same idea on both, so a team or a creator with two phones uses one workflow.
  • Two real files from the full sensor. Both formats come from the full 4:3 frame rather than one being cut out of the other.
  • Front & Back as separate files. You can cut between your face and the scene, grade them separately, or combine them later, instead of living with a layout chosen before you pressed record.

When is ContentCam not worth paying for?

  • You only post vertical video. The point of ContentCam is getting both formats from one take. If you never need a horizontal version, the free tier may be all you want, or your phone's camera may be enough.
  • You're on an older iPhone. ContentCam needs iOS 18, which means iPhone XS, XR or newer.
  • You want 60fps Front & Back on Android. On Android, Front & Back records HD at up to 30fps, on phones that can run both cameras together.

How do you test ContentCam before paying?

Download it, record a real video in Single Lens and check both files in your Photos app. Then use your 5 Front & Back and 5 Dual Lens recordings on the kind of video you actually make. If you want to try everything in Premium, start the annual plan's 7-day free trial and cancel in your App Store or Google Play subscriptions before it ends if it isn't for you. Your recordings stay on your phone either way.
