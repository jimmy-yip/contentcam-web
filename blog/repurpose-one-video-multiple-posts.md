# How to Repurpose One Video Into Multiple Posts (2026)

Source: https://contentcam.app/blog/repurpose-one-video-multiple-posts/

Repurposing starts at capture, not in the edit. What the crop costs (about 32% either way), how to build cut points while filming, and how to adapt one recording per platform.

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 12 September 2026

Repurposing gets treated as an editing job. By the time you are in the edit, most of the decisions are already made. A widescreen crop of a vertical clip keeps about 32% of the frame, and no editor puts back what the camera never recorded. The work happens before you press record.

## Where does repurposing actually start?

At capture, with two decisions: what you frame, and what format you record.

Everything downstream inherits those. If the subject sits in the left third of a wide shot, the vertical version loses it. If you shot 9:16 only, the widescreen version is a crop of a crop. An editor can cut, reorder, caption and grade. An editor cannot widen the frame.

## What does the crop actually cost?

About two thirds, in either direction.

Take a 16:9 frame and cut a full-height 9:16 shape out of it. The vertical keeps 81/256 of the original width, which is roughly 32%. Do it the other way, cropping a full-width 16:9 out of a 9:16 frame, and you keep about 32% of the height. The arithmetic is symmetrical and it is unforgiving.

Two things follow. Anything you place by the rule of thirds in a wide shot lands on or past the edge of the vertical one. And the safe working area for footage that has to serve both formats is the centre column, not the whole frame.

## What can one recording actually become?

More than most people take from it, if the recording was long enough and structured loosely.

- **One long-form piece** for YouTube, horizontal.
- **Three to six short clips**, each built around a single complete idea, vertical.
- **A carousel or a text post** from the transcript, which costs nothing extra.
- **B-roll** from the offcuts, reusable in future pieces.
- **Stills** pulled as frames for thumbnails and covers.

The constraint is rarely the footage. It is that the footage was shot as one continuous argument with no clean internal boundaries.

## How do you find the cut points?

Build them in while filming, rather than hunting for them afterwards.

The practical method is to speak in complete units. Make one point, finish the thought, pause for two full seconds, then start the next one. Those pauses become your cut points, and each unit becomes a standalone clip that does not need surrounding context to make sense.

The test for whether a segment can stand alone: does it open with something other than a pronoun or a connective? A clip that starts with "and that is why" or "so it does" cannot survive on its own. A clip that starts with the noun can.

## How do you adapt each cut for its platform?

Change the framing and the first three seconds. Leave the rest alone.

- **Vertical platforms** want the subject centred and the bottom third clear of anything important, because caption, username and buttons sit there.
- **Widescreen** tolerates off-centre composition and rewards it.
- **The opening seconds carry the weight.** The same clip with a different first line performs differently, and recutting an opener costs minutes rather than hours.

Re-exporting the same file at a different size is not repurposing. Reframing it and reopening it is.

## How do you make this a repeatable batch?

Film in blocks, log while you are still standing there, and cut in one sitting.

The habit that saves the most time is logging as you go. After each take, write one line: what it was, whether it was usable, roughly where it sits. Ten seconds each. Reviewing two hours of unlabelled footage later costs far more than the logging did.

Record ten seconds of silence at the end of a session as well. That gives you a clean sample of the room for noise reduction, and something to fill gaps with.

## Common questions

**Can you repurpose a vertical video for YouTube?** You can crop it, but you keep only about 32% of the height and you cannot recover the sides. Recording both formats at capture avoids the loss.

**How many posts can one filming session produce?** Realistically one long-form piece plus three to six short clips, if you spoke in self-contained units rather than one continuous take.

**Is it better to film once for everything or separately per platform?** Once, provided the framing survives both crops. Filming separately doubles the work and the footage rarely matches.

**What ruins a clip for repurposing?** Starting a thought mid-sentence, and putting the subject at the edge of a wide frame.

**Do I need to re-edit everything for each platform?** No. Reframe, and recut the opening seconds. The body of the edit carries over.
