## Which aspect ratios does UGC need?

Four shapes cover nearly every placement a brand can run a UGC video in. At 1080 pixels on the short side:

- **9:16, 1080×1920:** TikTok, Instagram Reels and Stories, YouTube Shorts.
- **4:5, 1080×1350:** feed placements on Instagram and Facebook.
- **1:1, 1080×1080:** square feed placements.
- **16:9, 1920×1080:** YouTube, product pages, websites and TV-style placements.

Check the brief before you film. If it only asks for 9:16, you may never need the rest, but a brand that likes the video will often come back and ask for another shape. Having a real 16:9 file at that point beats re-shooting.

## How much of the frame does each crop keep?

The answer depends on which file you crop from. Starting from a 9:16 vertical file (1080×1920) and from a 16:9 horizontal file (1920×1080):

| Target | From the 9:16 file | From the 16:9 file |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9:16 | 100% | 32% of the width |
| 4:5 | 70% of the height | 45% of the width |
| 1:1 | 56% of the height | 56% of the width |
| 16:9 | 32% of the height | 100% |

The pattern is clear. Cut 9:16, 4:5 and 1:1 from the vertical file, and use a real horizontal file for 16:9. The two corner cases, 16:9 from a vertical and 9:16 from a horizontal, both throw away about two thirds of the picture. The 16:9 strip from a vertical has to be enlarged about 3.2 times in area to fill a 1920×1080 timeline, which is where the softness comes from. ## How do you frame one UGC take for every ratio?

Keep everything that matters in the middle of the vertical frame. A 1:1 crop keeps only the middle 1080 of 1920 rows, so the top 420 and bottom 420 pixels are at risk. In practice:

- **Product at chest height,** held near the centre of the frame, not low at the bottom edge.
- **Eyes about a third of the way down** the vertical frame, not right at the top, so a 1:1 or 4:5 crop doesn't cut through your forehead.
- **Hands in the middle band** for unboxings and demos. Hands at the bottom edge disappear in the square cut.
- **No text on screen.** Brands add their own captions and offers per placement, and burned-in text gets cut differently in every ratio.
- **Room either side** for the horizontal version. In a real 16:9 file you should see the space around you, so tidy the background past where your vertical frame ends.

## How should you hand UGC files to a brand?

As separate, clearly named files, one per ratio, plus the raw takes if the contract includes them. A brand's editor should be able to tell what a file is without opening it. ContentCam names each recording after the project, scene and take number, with the shape at the end, for example skincare_hook_003_Portrait.mov and skincare_hook_003_Landscape.mov, so the pair stays together when you upload a folder.

Send the files through a link that keeps the originals, such as a shared cloud folder, rather than through a chat app that may compress them.

## FAQ

### What aspect ratio should UGC be?

Ask the brand, then film so you can deliver all four common shapes: 9:16 (1080×1920) for TikTok, Reels and Stories, 4:5 (1080×1350) and 1:1 (1080×1080) for feed placements, and 16:9 (1920×1080) for YouTube and websites.

### Can I crop a vertical UGC video to 16:9?

You can, but the 16:9 strip is only 1080×608 pixels, about 32% of the vertical frame, and it has to be enlarged about 3.2 times in area to fill a 1920×1080 timeline. It looks soft and tight. Record a real horizontal version instead.

### How do I film UGC for 4:5 and 1:1 as well?

Shoot vertical and keep the product, your face and your hands in the middle of the frame. A 4:5 crop keeps the middle 70% of a 9:16 frame's height and a 1:1 crop keeps the middle 56%, so anything near the top or bottom edge gets cut.
