How to Film Your Kid's Reaction and What They're Seeing at the Same Time (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 11 October 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026

To film your kid's reaction and what they're seeing in one take, record the front and back camera at the same time, with the phone in front of your child: screen facing your child, rear camera facing the giraffe, the cake or the first snow. ContentCam's Front & Back mode does this on any iPhone running iOS 18 (including iPhone 15 and 16) and on Android phones that support it, and saves your child's face and the scene as two separate files you can cut between or combine later. The other ways are Apple's Dual Capture on iPhone 17-family models, two phones, or Snapchat's Dual Camera.

Disclosure: I build ContentCam, so I'm not neutral. Apple and Snapchat facts below come from their own support and specs pages, checked 5 November 2026.

Which camera films my child's face?

The front (selfie) camera films whoever is facing the screen. Point the phone at your child the usual way and the rear camera gets their face while the giraffe behind you goes unfilmed.

To get the child's face and what the child sees, the phone goes between your child and the scene, screen towards your child, rear camera pointing where your child is looking:

  • Zoo glass or a fence: stand beside your child and hold the phone at their chest height, angled so the rear camera sees the enclosure and the selfie camera looks up at their face.
  • Pram: a phone clamp on the pram handlebar or frame, screen facing your child, rear camera facing forward down the path.
  • Birthday table: a small tripod on the table in front of your child, rear camera pointing at the door the cake comes through.

Keep the phone below your child's eyeline. A child watching their own face on the preview is not watching the penguins.

Which way should I use?

Use ContentCam on an iPhone 15 or 16, which has no Dual Capture, on an Android phone, or on any phone when you want the reaction and the scene as separate clips. Separate files let you show the giraffe full screen, then cut to your child's face at the gasp, or place the face where you like afterwards. If your iPhone is on Apple's Dual Capture list above and one fixed video is enough, Dual Capture is built in. Snapchat suits a quick Story. How close should the phone be to my child's face?

About an arm's length or closer. The selfie camera is wide, so a face two metres away becomes a small dot, and a picture-in-picture inset shrinks it again. Aim for your child's face filling at least a third of the selfie frame.

How do I get good sound of my kid's reaction?

Stay quiet. In every setup above the microphone is nearest your child, so the gasp and the laugh come through, unless the parent beside the phone is saying "look, look!". Wind fixes are in filming outdoors in wind, backlight and sun.

What about light at the zoo and indoors?

The two cameras face opposite ways, so they rarely share good light. Outdoors, keep the sun to one side: sun behind the scene floods the rear camera, sun behind your child shadows their face. In aquariums the lit tank lights your child's face; hold the rear lens close to the glass to cut reflections. At a birthday, turn a lamp on near your child before the lights go off for the candles.

Will it drain my battery?

Faster than normal video, because two cameras and the screen run together. Charge to full and start recording when the moment is close, not in the queue.

FAQ

Can an iPhone 15 or iPhone 16 film my kid's reaction and the scene at once?

Yes, in a third-party app. Apple's Dual Capture is only on iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPhone Air, iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max and iPhone Duo. ContentCam's Front & Back mode works on any iPhone running iOS 18, including iPhone 15 and iPhone 16, and saves the face and the scene as two separate files.

Does Snapchat Dual Camera work on Android?

Not yet. Snapchat Support says "Dual Camera will be available on Android soon" (checked 5 November 2026). On Android, use two phones, or a camera app that runs the front and rear cameras together on phones that support it.

Can I slow down my kid's reaction in a front and back video?

On an iPhone, yes, a little. ContentCam's Front & Back records both cameras at up to 60 fps on iPhones that support it (Premium), so you can slow the reaction to half speed. Apple's Dual Capture is capped at 30 fps, and ContentCam's Front & Back on Android records up to 30 fps. For deeper slow motion, film your child's face on a second phone in Slo-mo.
