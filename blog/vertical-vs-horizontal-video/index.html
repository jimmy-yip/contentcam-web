Vertical vs Horizontal Video: Which Should You Shoot? (2026)

By Jimmy, developer of ContentCam · 26 August 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026

Shoot vertical (9:16) when the video will play in a full-screen phone feed, and horizontal (16:9) when it will play in a landscape player: the main YouTube feed on a computer, a TV, a website. If it is going to both, don't pick one and crop it into the other later. The crop maths below shows how much that costs.

Which shape fills the screen on each platform?

The rule underneath every row: a video looks best when its shape matches the screen it plays on. Where I could check the platform's own help page, the row says so; the rest describe how the app displays video, not an official spec.

The split comes down to this: the fast feeds people swipe through on a phone are vertical, and the places people sit back and watch, or search and find a video months later, are horizontal.

What does cropping one shape into the other cost?

The usual way to "get both" is to shoot one shape and crop the other out of it in an editor. The maths is simple, and it's worse than most people guess. Keep the full height of a 16:9 frame and cut a 9:16 strip from it: the strip's width is the height times 9/16, and the height is already the width times 9/16. So the strip is (9/16) x (9/16) = 81/256 of the original width, which is 31.6%.

The lost picture isn't the only cost. Cut a 9:16 strip from a 1920 x 1080 file and you have 608 x 1080 pixels, which then get stretched 1.78 times on each side to fill a 1080 x 1920 vertical. The reverse crop does exactly the same thing. That stretch is why a cropped second version looks soft next to the original. Why cropping vertical to horizontal looks wrong covers the framing side of it.

Why the sensor changes the maths

A phone's main camera sensor is 4:3, the shape of its photos. It is wider than a 9:16 frame and taller than a 16:9 one. Cut each shape straight from the sensor and a 16:9 frame uses 75% of it, while a 9:16 frame uses 42.2%. Cut the 9:16 out of a 16:9 recording instead and you are down to 23.7% of the sensor, because the first crop has already thrown away the top and bottom.

That difference is the whole case for recording both shapes at once instead of cropping later. Common questions

Should I film vertical or horizontal?

Film vertical (9:16) if the video will play in a full-screen phone feed: TikTok, Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts. Film horizontal (16:9) if it will play in a landscape player: the main YouTube feed on a computer, a TV or a website. If it is going to both, record both shapes from the same take rather than cropping one into the other.

How much of the picture do you lose cropping 16:9 to 9:16?

About 68%. A 9:16 frame cut from the full height of a 16:9 frame is 81/256 of its width, which is 31.6%. From a 1920 x 1080 file that leaves a 608 x 1080 strip, which then has to be stretched 1.78 times on each side to fill a 1080 x 1920 vertical.

Can you post vertical video on the main YouTube channel?

Yes. YouTube Help says the standard aspect ratio on a computer is 16:9 and the player resizes to fit other shapes, but 9:16 vertical videos on computer browsers may get extra padding at the sides. Vertical works best as a Short, which YouTube accepts in a square or vertical aspect ratio up to 3 minutes long.

Is square video a safe middle ground?

Only partly. A 1:1 crop from 16:9 keeps 56.3% of the frame, much more than a 9:16 crop, and YouTube Shorts accepts square. But a square video does not fill a phone screen in a full-screen feed, so it gives up the main reason to shoot vertical.
